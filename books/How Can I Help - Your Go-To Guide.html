Preface

前言

I was eighteen weeks pregnant when my husband and I learned that our baby had a condition known as a diaphragmatic hernia —a condition very few children survive. Despite visits to numerous children’s hospitals before he was born, there was just no guarantee he was going to make it. So we finally let people know what we were facing: He was going to be born very sick, he was going to need surgery, and he might not survive.

我怀孕十八周时，我和丈夫得知我们的宝宝患有膈疝——一种极少有婴儿能够存活的疾病。尽管在宝宝出生前我们跑遍了多家儿童医院，但医生仍然无法保证他能够活下来。所以，我们最终决定告诉大家我们面临的困境：他出生时就会非常虚弱，需要接受手术，而且他可能无法存活。

Brandon was born on March 29, 1999. He was immediately transported to Pittsburgh’s Children’s Hospital, where he was eventually put on a heart/lung machine (ECHMO). Later, he had surgery to repair the hernia. These were the most grueling days of our lives. With another young son still at home, we traveled 120 miles daily to be at Brandon’s side. Since he had been born via C-section, I was not allowed to drive for a few weeks and had to rely on others to take me to see him. In addition, my husband had to return to work, and someone had to care for our two-year-old son at home.

布兰登出生于1999年3月29日。他出生后立即被送往匹兹堡儿童医院，最终上了体外膜肺氧合机（ECHMO）。之后，他又接受了疝气修复手术。那段日子是我们人生中最难熬的。由于家里还有一个年幼的儿子，我们每天要驱车120英里（约193公里）去看望布兰登。因为他是剖腹产，我出生后的几周内不能开车，只能依靠别人开车送我去看他。此外，我的丈夫必须回去上班，家里也需要有人照顾我们两岁的儿子。

We were blessed to have family who split their time sitting at Brandon’s bedside and caring for our son Trey at home so that we could visit the hospital. They were there for us when Brandon’s condition turned critical and we had to stay at the hospital by his side. In addition to transporting me to the hospital, a few friends visited us at the hospital to see our baby and to make sure we were doing OK.

我们很幸运，家人轮流在布兰登的病床边陪伴他，同时在家照顾我们的儿子特雷，这样我们才能去医院探望。布兰登病情危急，我们不得不留在医院守候时，他们一直都在我们身边。除了送我去医院，还有几位朋友来医院探望我们的孩子，也关心我们是否一切安好。

We were not OK. Our baby was fighting for his life, and we had another baby at home who missed his parents and could not understand why they were not at home. We were tired, stressed, heartsick, and had never felt more helpless in all our lives.

我们当时的情况糟透了。我们的宝宝正在与死神搏斗，家里还有一个小宝宝，他想念父母，不明白为什么他们不在家。我们疲惫不堪，压力巨大，心如刀绞，从未感到如此无助。

Brandon passed away after only twenty-nine days of life. His little body could not take any more. We left the hospital for the final time and headed straight to the funeral home to make final arrangements for our child. Never would we have expected that our first dealings with a funeral home would be to plan the transport and burial of our baby. It was one of the most horrible times imaginable for us.

布兰登出生仅仅二十九天就离开了人世。他幼小的身体再也承受不住了。我们最后一次离开医院，直接前往殡仪馆，为我们的孩子安排后事。我们做梦也没想到，我们与殡仪馆的第一次接触竟然是为了安排我们孩子的遗体运送和安葬。那是我们所能想象到的最痛苦的时刻之一。

The funeral took place, and then it was back to life—but it had changed so very much for our family. We would not be bringing home a newborn baby. Trey would not grow up with a baby brother. The dream we had for our life had suddenly been redefined. We were parents to two children, yet only one was alive.

葬礼结束后，生活又恢复了平静——但对我们家来说，一切都已彻底改变。我们再也无法迎接新生儿的到来。特雷再也无法拥有一个弟弟。我们对生活的憧憬突然被重新定义。我们本该是两个孩子的父母，却只有一个孩子活了下来。

No one ever expects to outlive their child, and Brandon’s death was something that was very difficult to understand, let alone live with. If it were not for the outpouring of love and support from our family and friends, I’m not sure we would have been able to get through this painful time as well as we did.

没有人会预料到自己会白发人送黑发人，布兰登的离世令人难以接受，更难以承受。如果没有家人和朋友给予的爱与支持，我们恐怕无法如此坚强地度过这段痛苦的时光。

Suddenly, it became very important for us to keep Brandon’s memory alive. We found ourselves members of a proverbial club we didn’t want to be in—the “parents who’ve lost a child” club. But we were so grateful to find that there were other “members” who knew exactly what we were going through, as we all shared a similar experience. We joined a support group called SHARE, which is for parents who have lost a baby due to miscarriage, stillbirth, or infant death.

1. Our Changing World

This book is probably needed more at this time in history than ever before. Times and circumstances have changed dramatically. Technology has made it easier for us to communicate with each other. We can now Skype or FaceTime through our computers and phones with people all over the world for free. And yet, at the same time, many of us have our heads so deeply buried in that same technology that it is actually detrimental to face-to-face communication. We are now able to chat with someone on the other side of the world, and yet we don’t even know our next door neighbor, let alone carry on a conversation with her.

在当今这个时代，这本书或许比以往任何时候都更加必要。时代和环境都发生了翻天覆地的变化。科技让我们的沟通变得更加便捷。如今，我们可以通过电脑和手机，免费地与世界各地的人们进行Skype或FaceTime视频通话。然而，与此同时，我们中的许多人却沉迷于科技，以至于面对面的交流反而受到了损害。我们现在可以与世界另一端的人聊天，却连隔壁邻居都不认识，更别提和她交谈了。

Our lives are made better by the relationships we have. The connections we have to one another are critical, yet too many times those connections are missing. As a result, some people have absolutely no idea what to do, what to say, or even how to broach the subject with someone who is experiencing a tragedy in their life.

人际关系让我们的生活更加美好。人与人之间的联系至关重要，然而很多时候，这种联系却缺失了。因此，当有人遭遇人生悲剧时，有些人完全不知道该做什么、该说什么，甚至不知道该如何与他们谈论这个话题。

Back in the day, when someone was dealing with a difficult situation, there were many sources of support to help them through it. A few of the strongest ones that

As a result, a lot of these support systems are no longer in place. It isn’t so much that people don’t care. It’s that times and the way we live our lives have changed. Let’s take a look at these traditional support systems and some of the key changes that have affected them.

我记忆中的童年生活里，有大家庭、邻居、朋友，还有教会的姐妹们。但如今，科技的进步彻底改变了我们许多人的沟通和互动方式。结果，许多这样的支持系统不复存在。这并非是人们漠不关心，而是时代变迁，生活方式也随之改变。让我们一起来看看这些传统的支持系统，以及影响它们的一些关键变化。

Extended Family

大家庭

It used to be that families lived in very close proximity to each other. When people needed help, they could rely on family members to pitch in and take care of things. Grandparents could watch the kids at home. Aunts could send over dinner. Uncles could mow the lawn.

过去，家人住得非常近。当人们需要帮助时，可以依靠家人帮忙照看东西。祖父母可以帮忙照看孩子，姑姑可以送饭过来，叔叔可以帮忙修剪草坪。

These days, many families are spread out all over the country. Family members are so far away they have to Skype to visit their grandchildren. So when a tragedy occurs, it’s not as simple as running next door or getting in your car and driving a few blocks to be able to pitch in and help. Now you have to plan trips by plane, train, and automobile—trips that can take anywhere from a few hours to a few days. 如今，许多家庭成员分散在全国各地。亲人远隔重洋，甚至需要通过Skype才能与孙辈见面。因此，一旦发生不幸，不再像以前那样，跑到隔壁或者开车几个街区就能赶来帮忙。现在，人们需要计划飞机、火车和汽车的行程——这些行程可能耗时数小时，也可能长达数天。有些人需要请假，而另一些人则可能没有时间或资源赶到现场提供帮助。

当家人相隔数百甚至数千英里时，阿姨很难过来陪孩子，单身母亲也很难放下一切赶回家照顾生病的父亲，与孩子分居两地的父亲也很难在前妻离开小镇时承担起照顾孩子的责任。

社区也发生了变化。许多人还能回忆起孩子们在人行道上骑车、邻居们坐在前廊的秋千或摇椅上、人们随意串门拜访的情景。过去，邻居们会互相照看孩子，街坊邻居的孩子们还会帮街尾的寡妇跑腿办事。

俗话说，“养育一个孩子需要整个村庄的力量”。但在当今世界，这比以往任何时候都更具挑战性。过去那些可能待在家照顾孩子的妈妈们，现在都外出工作赚钱了。孩子们过去常常和邻居的小伙伴们一起玩娃娃、踢球或捉迷藏，现在却没时间了；他们忙得不可开交，很少回家。而街尾那位寡妇，现在住在养老院，因为她的家人住得太远，无法照顾她。

如今，许多人能认识自己的邻居都算是幸运了。以下是许多美国人典型的一天：他们走到车库，上车，打开车库门，倒车出来，遥控关上车库门，然后去上班。下班后，他们直接开车进车库，走进家门。他们就这样往返于家和公司之间，从未与邻居有过任何接触。

人行道和前廊如今已被后院露台取代，这进一步加剧了人与人之间的隔阂。我们对隐私的需求实际上正在切断我们社区的生命线。

虽然这样的女性依然存在，但由于时间和金钱的限制，她们的人数已经很少了。如今，许多女性都有工作——有些是出于自愿，大多数是迫于无奈。在工作和照顾家庭之间，大多数女性根本不可能抽出一天时间，自愿为教区成员准备葬礼午宴。

虽然许多教会仍在继续这项事工，但很多教会却缺乏必要的资源。“教会妇女的职责”如今仅限于捐款，以便为悲痛的家庭提供餐食。虽然悲痛的家庭或许能吃饱，但却缺少了这项美好事工所提供的特别的关爱和体贴。此外，许多人不再信奉任何宗教，也不属于任何教会团体，当这些人遭遇不幸时，他们的支持系统可能会更加匮乏。

2. When a Loved One Dies

2. 当挚爱之人离世时

The thought of death can be overwhelming, scary, and even debilitating for many people. And because of this, some choose not to think about it at all. But this technique is not going to change the fact that everyone is going to die and that most likely, all of us will face losing someone we love.

对许多人来说，死亡的念头会让人感到难以承受、恐惧，甚至痛苦不堪。正因如此，有些人选择完全不去想它。但这种方法并不能改变每个人都会死亡的事实，而且我们大多数人最终都会面临失去挚爱的痛苦。

For those who have a difficult time even thinking about death, dealing with the death of a loved one can be catastrophic. Many people block it completely out of their minds because facing the finality of death is just too scary. Questions such as What happens when one dies? Is it painful? Where do you go? can make you feel scared and uncomfortable.

对于那些连思考死亡都感到困难的人来说，面对亲人的离世无疑是一场灾难。许多人选择彻底回避死亡，因为直面死亡的终极性实在太过可怕。诸如“人死后会发生什么？”、“会很痛苦吗？”、“死后会去哪里？”之类的问题，会让人感到恐惧和不安。

And when someone you love dies, you come to realize you will never see them, talk to them, or hold them ever again. It’s extremely painful to even think about, so imagine what it feels like when someone you love is facing such a loss. Imagine how much they might be hurting.

当你所爱的人离世时，你会意识到你再也无法见到他们、与他们交谈、拥抱他们。仅仅是想到这一点就令人无比痛苦，所以想象一下，当你所爱的人面临这样的失去时，你会作何感想？想象一下，他们该有多么伤心难过。

When someone loses a loved one, they are often not prepared for what is happening. For some, it is a sudden traumatic situation such as an unexpected death; their family member or friend was fine one minute and gone the next. It’s shocking, and often, those left behind are not only trying to process what has just happened, but also dealing with thoughts of what they might have left unsaid or unresolved with the person who has suddenly died. For others, it’s watching someone they love suffer, either from a debilitating condition or a long, lingering disease. So when death comes, it can seem to be a relief that the deceased person’s suffering is over. Those left behind might have had time to say everything they needed to say to the person who died, but still, they’re grieving.

当人们失去挚爱之人时，往往毫无准备。对一些人来说，这是突如其来的创伤，例如亲人猝然离世；他们的家人或朋友前一刻还好好的，下一刻就离开了人世。这令人震惊，而留下来的人不仅要努力消化刚刚发生的一切，还要面对那些可能与逝者之间未曾说出口或未曾解决的问题。对另一些人来说，则是眼睁睁看着自己所爱的人遭受病痛折磨，无论是身患重疾还是长期慢性疾病。因此，当死亡来临时，逝者似乎也解脱了，仿佛痛苦终于结束了。留下来的人或许已经来得及把所有想对逝者说的话都说完，但他们依然沉浸在悲痛之中。

Death is not a topic people should dwell on, yet it’s not something they should ignore. So when it happens, having someone to help them through their grieving is one of life’s greatest gifts. It’s a gift you can give them out of love and one that they will appreciate forever. You can give them that gift by showing up to help them bear the sorrow, letting them lean on you in weak moments, and talking with them about their loss.

死亡并非人们应该过多谈论的话题，但也并非应该忽视。因此，当死亡来临时，有人陪伴他们度过悲伤，是人生中最珍贵的礼物之一。这是出于爱而给予的礼物，他们会永远感激。你可以通过陪伴他们，帮助他们承受悲痛，在他们脆弱的时候让他们依靠你，并与他们倾诉失去亲人的痛苦，来给予他们这份礼物。

When people suffer a loss, they are dealing with feelings of grief, denial, anger, sadness, and confusion. Some are experiencing emotions they have never before encountered, and most of them are terrified. That is why it is so important to offer them all the love, care, and support you can.

当人们遭遇失去亲人或挚爱之人时，他们会经历悲伤、否认、愤怒、难过和困惑等种种情绪。有些人甚至会经历从未体验过的情感，而大多数人都会感到恐惧。正因如此，给予他们尽可能多的爱、关怀和支持才显得尤为重要。

I have had the opportunity to talk with people who have lost loved ones to illnesses, suicide, and accidents. They have so many stories to share of things their friends and loved ones did to help them navigate the waters of shock and sorrow. So what can you do or say that will help a loved one deal with death? Here are some specific tips and suggestions from people who shared their stories with me.

我有幸与一些因疾病、自杀或意外失去亲人的人们交谈。他们分享了许多朋友和亲人如何帮助他们走出震惊和悲痛的故事。那么，我们能做些什么或说些什么来帮助亲人面对死亡呢？以下是一些来自与我分享过他们故事的人们的具体建议和提示。

Be There

务必到场

Every single person surveyed for this book listed “being there” as what was most important to them. If you are close to the person who is suffering from a loss or other tragedy, just being with them so they are not alone is not only important but crucial in helping them through this unsettled time of their life.

所有参与本书调查的人都将“陪伴”列为他们最看重的事情。如果你身边有亲近的人正在经历失去亲人或其他不幸，陪伴他们，让他们不感到孤单，不仅重要，而且对于帮助他们度过这段动荡不安的时期至关重要。

Your presence is all that is required. No words, no actions—just BE.

你只需在场即可。无需言语，无需行动——只需存在。

Be present for them, listen to them, hold their hand, let them cry, or cry with them. When you are with them, they know they do not have to face their loss alone. Some may want to talk and others may not, but all agree that knowing someone is there is very important.

陪伴在他们身边，倾听他们的心声，握住他们的手，让他们哭泣，或者和他们一起哭泣。当你陪伴在他们身边时，他们会知道自己不必独自面对失去亲人的痛苦。有些人可能想倾诉，有些人可能不想，但他们都认同，知道有人在身边是多么重要。

Jody, who lost her sister to breast cancer, says she was blessed with the love and support she received from her friends. “My lifelong best friend from Atlanta showed up the day after my sister passed and stayed with me throughout her funeral. She took care of getting the food together since everyone congregated at my house, kept the house straightened up, and was there in the middle of the night when I would fall apart.” She adds, “I really had exceptional friends that left nothing unsaid or undone for us.”

乔迪的妹妹因乳腺癌去世，她说自己很幸运能得到朋友们的爱和支持。“我来自亚特兰大的挚友，在我妹妹去世的第二天就赶来了，一直陪着我直到葬礼结束。因为大家都聚集在我家，她负责准备食物，把家里收拾得井井有条，而且在我半夜崩溃的时候，她也总是在我身边。”她补充道，“我真的拥有非常棒的朋友，他们为我们做了所有该做的事，说了那么多话，做了那么多事。”

Judy says the sudden death of her husband was the most traumatic experience of her life. She is grateful for a dear friend who immediately traveled hundreds of miles to be at her side. She says, “She took over my entire life and allowed me to rest and try to sleep and use her as a sounding board through all my ranting and monologues that I spouted endlessly.”

朱迪说，丈夫的猝然离世是她一生中最痛苦的经历。她非常感激一位挚友，这位朋友第一时间从数百英里之外赶来陪伴她。她说：“她完全接管了我的生活，让我能够休息、入睡，并倾听我没完没了的抱怨和独白。”

Jody offers this advice for helping others dealing with the death of a loved one: “Let them know you’re there; get them out among the living—be relentless if necessary. And do not disappear after the funeral—that is when the real loss sets in and precisely when people need their loved ones the most.”

乔迪就如何帮助其他正在经历亲人离世的人提出了以下建议：“让他们知道你一直都在；让他们多接触其他人——必要时可以坚持不懈。葬礼结束后不要消失——那时才是真正失去亲人的痛苦开始显现的时候，也是人们最需要亲人陪伴的时候。”

Brady, who lost her sister Erin to cancer, agrees that keeping in touch after the funeral is so important. “Once the funeral is over, everyone stops coming around and calling. I know everyone must move along and get on with life, but the people who did still call and keep in touch really meant a lot.”

布雷迪的妹妹艾琳因癌症去世，她也认为葬礼后保持联系非常重要。“葬礼一结束，大家就都不再来串门打电话了。我知道每个人都必须继续生活，但那些仍然打电话联系的人真的让我很感动。”

Be prepared that your friend may not be himself or herself. Trying to process losing someone he or she loves can be mind-boggling. Some people feel as though their world has been turned upside down, and their focus therefore won’t always be on what is going on around them. Some people lose all track of time in the first few days. They become confused about the time of day, the day of the week, or even the month of the year. They may not know if it’s Monday or Tuesday or if it’s morning or night: all they know is that someone is there with them. But when they look back at that confusing time, the one thing they will be able to recall is whether they were feeling abandoned and alone or whether they were feeling grateful for being supported in a loving manner.

要做好心理准备，你的朋友可能状态不太好。失去挚爱之人，这种痛苦会让人难以承受。有些人感觉天翻地覆，注意力无法集中在周围发生的事情上。有些人会在最初的几天里完全失去时间概念，搞不清今天是几点、星期几，甚至一年中的哪个月份。他们可能不知道今天是星期一还是星期二，也不知道是早上还是晚上：他们唯一知道的是，有人陪伴在他们身边。但当他们回首那段迷茫的时光时，唯一能回忆起来的，是当时自己是感到被抛弃和孤独，还是因为得到了充满爱意的支持而心怀感激。

Listen

听

When people suffer a loss, they tend to run it through their minds almost endlessly; part of the grieving process is talking about it over and over and over. Some might want to tell you how it happened or how their loved one was discovered, while others might recall a favorite memory.

人们在经历失去亲人的痛苦时，往往会反复回忆这件事；哀悼过程的一部分就是一遍又一遍地谈论它。有些人可能想告诉你事情是如何发生的，或者亲人是如何被发现的，而另一些人则可能回忆起一段美好的往事。

Please remember to be patient with them. Keep in mind that your grieving friends or relatives are going through a very traumatic time. They’re trying to make sense of the fact that just yesterday, their loved one was alive, but today, he or she is dead. It could have been a sudden death, which still seems impossible to believe, or it could have been a long, drawn-out process about which every detail from diagnosis to death is crystal clear. Just let your grieving friends talk, process, and remember, because having someone there to listen without interruption is very important and always remembered later. Almost every person who responded to the survey listed a friend or loved one who would simply listen as one of the things they needed most.

请记住，要对他们保持耐心。要知道，你那些悲痛欲绝的朋友或亲人正经历着一段非常痛苦的时期。他们难以接受亲人昨天还活着，今天却已离世的事实。这可能是一场猝然离世，至今仍令人难以置信；也可能是一个漫长的过程，从确诊到死亡的每一个细节都清晰可见。请让你的朋友们倾诉、消化和回忆，因为有人在身边倾听而不打断他们非常重要，而且这份情谊日后也总是会被铭记。几乎所有参与调查的人都表示，他们最需要的就是一位能够倾听他们心声的朋友或亲人。

Lori, who lost a childhood friend to suicide, said, “Just listening helps a lot. Listen and let them get it out.” Whether it’s in person, by phone, or by Skype, let them do the talking and just lend an ear.

洛里曾因一位儿时好友自杀而失去她，她说：“倾听真的很有帮助。倾听，让他们把心里话说出来。”无论是当面、电话还是Skype，都让他们畅所欲言，你只需要认真倾听。

Don’t be surprised to find people experiencing many emotions all at once. Those who are grieving can express anger, sadness, and guilt even as they are laughing. It is not your job to try to help them calm down—just be there while they “feel” their way through these conflicting emotions so they can get them out of their system.

不要惊讶于人们同时经历多种情绪。悲伤的人即使在笑的时候，也可能同时表达愤怒、悲伤和内疚。你的职责不是试图让他们平静下来——只需陪伴在他们身边，让他们感受这些矛盾的情绪，并将它们释放出来。

You needn’t worry that you don’t know what to say; sometimes, no words are necessary. In fact, Jody put it this way: “There are not many people who are going to offer profound enough words to ease the pain. Don’t feel you need to do this. A simple and sincere ‘I’m so sorry!’ is often enough.”

你不必担心不知道该说什么；有时候，无需言语。事实上，乔迪是这么说的：“很少有人能说出足够深刻的话语来抚慰伤痛。别觉得你需要这么做。一句简单真诚的‘我很抱歉！’往往就足够了。”

Again, for those who grieve, repeating things may be necessary; just let them talk, process, and remember.

再说一遍，对于悲伤的人来说，重复某些事情可能是必要的；就让他们倾诉、消化和回忆吧。

Send Food and Drinks

送些食物和饮料。

When there is a death, many decisions have to be made. They range from buying a cemetery plot to writing an obituary, from picking out clothes for the deceased to dealing with the funeral arrangements. Remembering to eat, let alone deciding what to eat, rarely comes to mind. Sending a meal to the house of your loved one really helps because it gives them one less detail to attend to. It can be a favorite recipe for some immediate comfort food or a casserole that can be frozen and used at a later date. If you don’t cook, buy a bucket of chicken and some macaroni salad, send a luncheon tray with buns and condiments, or deliver a vegetable or fruit tray. Having prepared food on hand allows easy access to a meal at any time of day.

亲人离世后，需要做很多决定，从购买墓地到撰写讣告，从挑选逝者的衣物到安排葬礼事宜，不一而足。人们往往忽略了吃饭这件事，更别提决定吃什么了。给亲人送一份餐食真的很有帮助，因为这能让他们少操一份心。可以是他们最爱的现成菜肴，也可以是可以冷冻起来以后再吃的砂锅菜。如果您不会做饭，可以买一桶炸鸡和一些通心粉沙拉，或者送一份包含面包和调味品的午餐盒，又或者送一份蔬菜水果拼盘。提前准备好食物，就能让他们随时都能享用。

Another suggestion that not many think of is breakfast food. Sending a breakfast casserole or a tray of Danish makes it convenient for the family to eat something first thing in the morning. Your thoughtfulness can help get them through what likely will be a very long day. When Marianne lost her mother, one of the things she appreciated the most was the breakfast basket that a friend sent to her. “It had everything in the basket—napkins, individual cereals, milk, muffins, flavored coffee, and flavored cream cheese, along with a bouquet of flowers for my table.”

另一个很多人可能想不到的建议是送早餐。送一份早餐砂锅或一盘丹麦酥饼，就能方便家人早上起来吃点东西。你的这份心意能帮助他们度过可能漫长的一天。玛丽安失去母亲时，她最感激的礼物之一就是朋友送来的早餐篮。“篮子里应有尽有——餐巾纸、独立包装的麦片、牛奶、松饼、风味咖啡、风味奶油奶酪，还有一束鲜花放在我的餐桌上。”

If a lot of food is being sent to the house, you may want to consider drinks and ice. With guests streaming in and out of the house during this time, there is always the need for a cold drink or a cup of coffee.

如果送来的食物很多，你可能需要考虑准备一些饮料和冰块。这段时间客人来来往往，总会需要一杯冷饮或咖啡。

It also helps to include cups, napkins, plates, and eating utensils. Not only will these be needed, but they’ll be most appreciated when it’s time to clean up. Simply throwing everything away is much easier than having dishes and glasses stacking up.

最好也准备一些杯子、餐巾纸、盘子和餐具。这些不仅在用餐时会用到，而且在收拾的时候会非常方便。与其让碗碟杯子堆积如山，不如直接把所有东西都扔掉来得轻松得多。

Many families have one-day funeral viewings with perhaps a short one- or two-hour break between sessions. You may want to call the funeral home and see if you can send over food and/or drinks for the family to help them through their day.

许多家庭会安排一天的遗体告别仪式，中间可能只安排一到两个小时的短暂休息。您可以致电殡仪馆，询问是否可以送些食物和/或饮料给家属，帮助他们度过这段艰难的时光。

Pray

祈祷

If you are a person of faith, sending up a prayer for your loved one is something you can do anytime, anywhere. Most people surveyed said that just knowing others were praying for them gave them strength.

如果你有信仰，无论何时何地，你都可以为你的亲人祈祷。大多数受访者表示，仅仅是知道有人在为他们祈祷，就给了他们力量。

Anne, who lost her father, said this about prayer: “That someone would care enough to pray is overwhelming, and sometimes when you’re experiencing traumatic times, you have no ability to pray yourself. So that’s the time you desperately need the prayers of others.”

安妮失去了父亲，她这样评价祈祷：“有人愿意为你祈祷，这令人感动不已。有时，当你经历创伤性的时刻，你根本无法自己祈祷。所以，在那种时候，你迫切需要别人的祈祷。”

Nancy, who tragically lost her father, mother, and brother all within a few years of each other, says that knowing others are praying for you is very important. “It makes me know people want to help, even if there is nothing they can do physically. God will do what needs to be done, but it is nice to know you have others in your corner.”

南希在短短几年内接连失去了父亲、母亲和哥哥，她说，知道有人在为自己祈祷非常重要。“这让我知道人们想要帮助我，即使他们无法提供实际帮助。上帝会安排一切，但知道有人在身边支持我，感觉真的很好。”

Many others say knowing people are praying provides comfort and restores hope.

许多人表示，知道有人在祈祷能给人带来安慰，并重燃希望。

Send Condolences

致以慰问

Sending sympathy cards is one way to show someone you’re thinking of them. A card is something tangible one can read over again and perhaps keep in a special place. Make sure to include a small note when you send a card; it makes it so much more personal than just signing your name. Simply letting the person know you are thinking of them or jotting down a special memory of the deceased adds extra meaning to this simple act of caring.

寄送慰问卡是表达思念的一种方式。卡片是实实在在的，可以反复阅读，或许还能珍藏在某个特别的地方。寄卡时，别忘了附上一张小纸条；这比仅仅签名更显真挚。简单地告诉对方你在想念他们，或者写下一些关于逝者的美好回忆，都能让这份简单的关怀更添意义。

As technology has advanced, electronic condolences have gained popularity. Many funeral homes now have websites through which messages can be sent to the family of the deceased. Other people reach out via social networks to convey sympathy. Is one method more “proper” than the other? It depends on whom you ask. The majority of survey respondents said they preferred receiving traditional cards and notes. They said getting a card or note in the mail was much more meaningful because of the extra effort the person took to shop for, write, stamp, and mail it. In addition, many said they still have many of these notes and cards as sentimental keepsakes. A few of the respondents said they felt receiving an e-mail was just a bit too impersonal.

随着科技进步，电子慰问越来越受欢迎。许多殡仪馆现在都设有网站，方便人们向逝者家属发送慰问信息。也有人通过社交网络表达哀悼。哪种方式更“得体”呢？这取决于你问的是谁。大多数受访者表示，他们更喜欢收到传统的卡片和慰问信。他们认为，收到纸质卡片或慰问信更有意义，因为寄信人需要花费额外的心思去挑选、书写、贴邮票和寄送。此外，许多人表示，他们至今仍保留着许多这样的卡片和慰问信，作为珍贵的纪念品。只有少数受访者表示，他们觉得收到电子邮件显得有些冷冰冰的。

A growing number of people find a sympathetic e-mail acceptable and believe it’s definitely better than not receiving anything. However, this opinion in favor of electronic condolences was expressed mostly by younger people who have grown up communicating in this manner. Regardless of your opinion on its appropriateness, there can also be a drawback to this method—the receiver may not get your condolence message at all if it lands in a junk mail file.

越来越多的人认为收到慰问邮件是可以接受的，并且相信这总比什么都没收到要好。然而，这种支持电子慰问的观点主要来自年轻人，他们从小就习惯了这种沟通方式。无论你认为这种方式是否合适，它也存在一个缺点——如果你的慰问邮件被误判为垃圾邮件，收件人可能根本收不到。

Call

称呼

Making a phone call is a great way to show you care, but many avoid this action because they’re afraid they won’t know what to say or that they might say the wrong thing. You should not let that stop you because those phone calls are appreciated. Chances are the person won’t be up for a conversation, but your reaching out will mean a lot to them regardless.

打电话是表达关心的好方法，但很多人却因为害怕不知道该说什么或怕说错话而不敢打电话。你不应该因此而放弃，因为这些电话会让他们感到温暖。对方可能并不想聊天，但你的关心对他们来说意义非凡。

Many times, the person will not even answer the phone. If that’s the case, just leave a quick message letting them know you are thinking of them. You can offer some kind of help or assistance, or you can just let them know you will check back with them a little later. And then do just that. Follow up with another phone call in a few days.

很多时候，对方根本不会接电话。如果遇到这种情况，就简单地留言，告诉他们你在想念他们。你可以提供一些帮助或建议，或者只是告诉他们你会稍后再联系他们。然后就真的会联系他们。几天后再打个电话跟进一下。

Many times, people who reach out via the phone may perceive a lack of response as a rejection. That is most likely not the case. Not everyone grieves the same way. While some might want to talk about their loved one or what they’re experiencing, others might just not have the strength to tell their story one more time. Remember this is not about you; it’s about helping someone you love and care about to navigate through a very difficult time. Don’t worry about bothering them. As long as you keep your calls brief and you’re not calling all the time, your efforts will be appreciated.

很多时候，打电话寻求帮助的人如果得不到回应，可能会觉得受到了拒绝。但事实并非如此。每个人的悲伤方式都不一样。有些人可能想倾诉失去亲人的痛苦，或者倾诉自己的经历，而有些人可能已经精疲力竭，无法再次讲述自己的故事。记住，这不是为了你自己，而是为了帮助你所爱的人度过这段艰难的时期。不要担心会打扰到他们。只要你每次通话简短，不要频繁拨打，你的关心和帮助都会让他们感激不尽。

Other Helpful Suggestions

其他有用的建议

Here is a list of other things you may want to consider doing to help someone who is dealing with a death in their family:

以下是一些您可以考虑采取的其他措施，以帮助那些正在经历亲人去世的人：

• Shop for groceries. You can make a list of basic items like bread, milk, cereal, laundry detergent, toilet paper, etc., and go to the store and purchase them—or you can ask what your friend needs and fill out a list together. Sometimes, in the throes of grief, people are not thinking clearly, so don’t be surprised if they’re not even sure what they need. You might want to check with other family members or do some checking yourself. Then have the items delivered or deliver them yourself to their home.

• 购买生活必需品。您可以列出一些基本物品，例如面包、牛奶、麦片、洗衣液、卫生纸等等，然后去商店购买——或者您可以询问朋友需要什么，然后一起列一张清单。有时，人们在悲痛之中思维混乱，所以如果他们甚至不确定自己需要什么，也不要感到惊讶。您可以询问其他家庭成员，或者自己先确认一下。然后，您可以安排送货上门，或者亲自送到他们家中。

• Run errands. Do relatives need to be picked up at the airport? Do clothes need a trip to the dry cleaner? Are there beds to be made? Can you offer an extra bedroom for out-of-town relatives? These are the kinds of extra things that may be overlooked during this time. How nice it would be if there were someone who not only thought about those details but who would take care of them too!

• 跑腿办事。亲戚需要去机场接吗？衣服需要送去干洗吗？床铺需要整理吗？能否为外地来的亲戚提供额外的卧室？这些都是这段时间容易被忽略的小事。如果有人不仅能想到这些细节，还能帮你处理好，那该多好啊！

• Babysit. Sometimes, it’s difficult to talk with guests while tending to small children during visitation hours. Offering to watch over the children at the funeral home could be a big help. You could either entertain the children with crayons, paper, and coloring books in a back room of the funeral parlor or see if the funeral home has the equipment to let children watch a movie. You could also offer to keep the children in your home while folks are at the funeral home.

• 帮忙照看孩子。有时，在吊唁期间，一边照顾小孩一边与来宾交谈会很困难。主动提出在殡仪馆照看孩子会很有帮助。您可以带孩子们去殡仪馆后面的房间，用蜡笔、纸和涂色书让他们玩耍，或者看看殡仪馆是否有设备播放电影。您也可以提出在来宾们前往殡仪馆期间，把孩子寄养在您家。

• Care for pets. Does your friend or loved one have a pet? Is it being fed, walked, and taken outside to relieve itself? You could offer to take care of this.

• 照顾宠物。你的朋友或亲人养宠物吗？他们是否喂养、遛狗、带宠物外出排便？你可以主动提出帮忙照顾宠物。

• Make phone calls. Sometimes, friends and relatives from out of town need to be notified at the time of death and again once the arrangements have been made. You could offer to make some of those calls.

• 打电话。有时，需要通知外地的亲朋好友，在亲人去世时以及丧葬安排妥当后都需要再次通知。您可以主动提出帮忙拨打这些电话。

• Do housecleaning. Relatives may be coming in from out of town. You can offer to clean the bathrooms or make up the beds in the guest room to help prepare the house for visitors. These are all things that need to be done and are often not thought about until it’s time to go to bed. And there will be more cleaning up to do once the funeral is over and the guests have left town. A house full of eating, drinking, and visiting people can add up to a lot of mess to deal with once everyone has gone. You may also consider hiring a company to come in and clean the home after everything is over.

• 做些家务。亲戚们可能会从外地来。您可以主动提出打扫浴室或整理客房床铺，为迎接客人做好准备。这些都是需要做的事情，但往往要等到临睡前才会想起。葬礼结束后，客人离开后，还有更多的清洁工作要做。家里人吃喝玩乐、来来往往，一旦大家都走了，就会留下很多脏乱。您也可以考虑聘请专业的清洁公司在一切结束后进行彻底的清洁。

• Do laundry. You can check to see if laundry is piled up and offer to do a load or two. If people are in from out of town, there will be towels, bedclothes, and other things that need to be laundered.

• 帮忙洗衣服。你可以看看有没有什么脏衣服堆积起来，主动提出帮忙洗一两桶。如果有外地来的人，肯定会有毛巾、床单和其他东西需要清洗。

• House-sit. Many people like to deliver food for the family to eat after the funeral. Having someone at the house allows people to drop off food and other items for the family at any time. In addition to that, having someone at the house can prevent robberies. It’s a shame that those who are grieving even have to think about it, but it’s a fact that criminals comb through the obituaries to find a house with no one at home. Many families have been known to return from the cemetery only to find they have been robbed.

• 看家。许多人喜欢在葬礼后为家属送餐。有人在家可以方便人们随时送来食物和其他物品。此外，有人在家还能防止盗窃。对于正在悲痛中的人们来说，还要考虑这些事情，实在令人难过。但事实是，犯罪分子会仔细查看讣告，寻找无人居住的房屋下手。很多家庭从墓地回来后才发现家中已被盗。

• Create a gift. Use your gifts or talents to create a lasting memory. Because my son Brandon was just a month old when he passed away, he had very few belongings and so each of them had incredible meaning for his father and me. A family friend went to his woodshop and created the most beautiful memory box for us. It is a treasure that holds the few objects that remind us of our son. Another friend who was an artist sent a print of a beautiful tree in full bloom that adds beauty to our home. I look at it daily and am reminded of his personal act of kindness and compassion. I use my gift of jewelry making to create keepsake bracelets with healing natural stones and angel wing charms for friends and family members mourning a loved one. Use the gifts and talents you have to create a keepsake or something that can bring a smile to the face of someone going through so much pain.

• 制作一份礼物。用你的天赋或才能创造一份永恒的回忆。我的儿子布兰登去世时才一个月大，他几乎没有留下任何遗物，所以每一件都对我和他父亲来说意义非凡。一位朋友去他的木工坊为我们制作了一个精美的纪念盒。它珍藏着我们为数不多的、能让我们想起儿子的物品。另一位朋友是一位艺术家，他送了一幅盛开的树木的版画，为我们家增添了美感。我每天都会欣赏它，并想起他那份真挚的善意和同情。我用我的珠宝制作天赋，为正在哀悼亲人的亲朋好友制作带有疗愈能量的天然宝石和天使翅膀吊坠的纪念手链。用你的天赋和才能，制作一份纪念品，或者一些能让正在经历巨大痛苦的人们脸上露出笑容的东西。

After the Funeral

葬礼之后

Being with a friend throughout the funeral process is very important, but many say it is after the funeral when friendship can mean the most. When a family member dies, there is the shock and sorrow of the death, coupled with all the funeral arrangements that have to be made in a very short time. Family members can help with a lot of the planning, and friends can be around to provide food and help at this time. But once the funeral is over, the food has been eaten, the kitchen has been cleaned up, and family and friends have returned to their homes, reality sets in. Many people say the death really never hits them until everything is over. Some may need time to grieve in peace, which is very normal. However, loneliness can set in rather quickly, and they might need a friend more then than at any other time.

在葬礼期间有朋友陪伴固然重要，但许多人认为，葬礼结束后，友谊的意义才真正凸显。当亲人离世时，除了震惊和悲痛之外，还要在短时间内安排好所有葬礼事宜。家人可以帮忙筹划，朋友们也可以在这个时候提供食物和帮助。但葬礼结束后，饭菜吃完，厨房收拾干净，亲朋好友各自回家后，现实便会袭来。许多人说，直到一切结束，他们才真正感受到死亡的冲击。有些人需要时间独自哀悼，这很正常。然而，孤独感可能会很快袭来，他们或许比以往任何时候都更需要朋友的陪伴。

So what can you do to help your friend or loved one once everything is over and they find themselves alone? Here are some suggestions:

那么，当一切结束后，当你的朋友或爱人孤身一人时，你能做些什么来帮助他们呢？以下是一些建议：

• Make a phone call. Making a short phone call to simply check in on your friend can be very helpful. This lets them know you are thinking of them and allows you to find out if they need anything and to gauge how they’re doing. If you have the time and they’re in a talking mood, allow them to tell their story. You can also leave a message on their machine. On the other hand, if you find yourself calling frequently and notice they’re not answering at all, you may want to check on them with a visit or talk to a family member about your concerns.

• 打个电话。给朋友打个简短的电话问候一下会很有帮助。这能让他们知道你在想念他们，也能让你了解他们是否需要什么帮助，并大致了解他们的近况。如果你有时间，而且他们也愿意说话，不妨让他们说说自己的故事。你也可以在他们的留言机上留言。另一方面，如果你发现自己频繁打电话却始终无人接听，你或许应该亲自去拜访一下，或者和家人谈谈你的担忧。

• Invite them to dinner. Having dinner and conversation with a grieving friend is a great way to help them. It’s a night they don’t have to cook, and it gets them out of the house and engaged with other people for a while. In addition, someone who is grieving often does not feel like eating, let alone cooking, and they may dread cooking now because they are cooking for one less person or because the person who used to do the cooking is no longer there. Whether you want to offer a good home-cooked meal at your home or a night out at their favorite restaurant, your thoughtfulness will be greatly appreciated. If you live close by, you might even offer to send over dinner a few nights a week or organize a group of friends to pitch in.

• 邀请他们共进晚餐。与悲伤的朋友一起吃饭聊天是帮助他们的好方法。这样他们就不用做饭了，还能暂时离开家，与其他人交流。此外，悲伤的人往往没有胃口，更别提做饭了，他们现在可能更害怕做饭，因为家里少了一个人，或者因为以前负责做饭的人已经不在了。无论你是想在家为他们准备一顿丰盛的家常菜，还是带他们去他们最喜欢的餐厅，你的体贴都会让他们非常感激。如果你住得近，你甚至可以每周送几次饭过去，或者组织一群朋友一起帮忙。

• Keep in touch. Don’t let your initial outreach be the last contact you make with your grieving friend or loved one. Let them know you are still thinking of them and want them to heal. You can make a quick call, send a short note, or make plans to visit. Be patient and persistent. It may take weeks or months for someone to feel like getting back into the swing of life, but it will eventually happen.

• 保持联系。不要让最初的关心成为你与悲伤中的朋友或亲人之间的最后一次联系。让他们知道你仍然惦记着他们，希望他们早日康复。你可以打个电话，发个简短的便条，或者安排时间去探望。要有耐心，坚持不懈。一个人可能需要几周甚至几个月的时间才能重新融入生活，但这终究会实现的。

• Remember the dates. The first year after a death is very difficult for many people to navigate. It is the first time Christmas will be celebrated without a loved one—or it may be the first time ever that, because of their loss, they don’t celebrate Christmas at all. The first Mother’s Day or Father’s Day without Mom or Dad can be very depressing, but these days will be quite excruciating for a mom or dad who recently lost a child. The departed loved one’s birthday will pass without a celebration, and then comes the one-year anniversary of the death. For someone suffering from a loss, these are days that are full of mixed emotions. They become dreaded dates on the calendar, and when they do arrive, many people are flooded with memories and strong feelings.

• 记住这些日子。亲人去世后的第一年对许多人来说都非常难熬。这是他们第一次在没有挚爱之人陪伴的情况下庆祝圣诞节——或者，这可能是他们有生以来第一次因为失去亲人而完全放弃庆祝圣诞节。第一个没有父母陪伴的母亲节或父亲节可能会令人非常沮丧，但对于刚刚失去孩子的父母来说，这些日子更是痛苦不堪。逝者的生日会在没有庆祝的情况下悄然过去，然后便是逝世一周年纪念日。对于正在经历丧亲之痛的人来说，这些日子充满了复杂的情绪。它们成了日历上令人恐惧的日子，当这些日子真正到来时，许多人都会被回忆和强烈的情感所淹没。

During that first year, I was never so touched as when relatives sent us cards on Brandon’s birthday. They remembered! My sister still to this day sends a card every year to let us know she has never forgotten him. This one small gesture on her part means the world to my husband and me. We also have friends who have sent flowers or plants on the anniversary of Brandon’s death to let us know they are thinking of us. These kind acts of theirs will stay with us forever.

在那一年里，最让我感动的是亲戚们在布兰登生日那天寄来的贺卡。他们竟然还记得！直到今天，我的妹妹每年都会寄一张贺卡，告诉我们她从未忘记他。她这小小的举动对我和我丈夫来说意义非凡。我们还有一些朋友会在布兰登的忌日送来鲜花或盆栽，表达他们的思念。他们的这些善举将永远铭刻在我们心中。

Not-So-Helpful Actions

不太有益的行为

While most people I talked with said they had been overwhelmingly blessed with the comfort and support they received from family and friends after a death, they also said that family and friends said, did, or omitted a few things in ways that were more hurtful than helpful. Some were gracious enough to share these painful experiences with me in an effort to help others avoid similar discomfort.

虽然我采访的大多数人都表示，在亲人去世后，他们得到了家人和朋友的安慰和支持，这让他们感到无比幸运，但他们也提到，家人和朋友的一些言行或疏忽，反而让他们更加痛苦。有些人很宽容地与我分享了这些痛苦的经历，希望能帮助其他人避免类似的困扰。

Here are some of the actions that were more hurtful than helpful. We’ll talk about unhelpful words in the next section.

以下是一些弊大于利的行为。我们将在下一节讨论那些无益的言语。

Not Showing Up for the Visitation and/or the Funeral

未出席遗体告别仪式和/或葬礼

When Marianne lost her mother, she looked for certain people to be there for her. Some were; some weren’t.

玛丽安失去母亲后，她希望某些人能陪伴在她身边。有些人陪伴了她，有些人则没有。

Don’t base your decision to attend a funeral or wake on how well you knew the deceased. Base it on the relationship you have with their loved ones. Many people believe that the funeral is more for the surviving loved ones because they need closure and the support of their family, friends, and the members of their community.

不要根据你与逝者的熟识程度来决定是否参加葬礼或守灵仪式，而应该根据你与逝者亲属的关系来决定。许多人认为，葬礼更多的是为了生者亲属，因为他们需要告别亲人，需要家人、朋友和社区成员的支持。

If there is a legitimate reason you can’t make it to the funeral home or the wake, make sure to let your friend or relative know why you won’t be there. Simply not showing up can lead people to come to their own conclusions as to why you weren’t there—one of the main reasons they’ll come up with in their own minds is that they didn’t matter enough to you. That can be very painful at a time when they find themselves vulnerable. Marianne said it really meant a lot to see how many people cared about her when they took the time to stop to pay their respects to her mother.

如果您有正当理由无法前往殡仪馆或守灵仪式，请务必告知您的朋友或亲人原因。仅仅因为没有露面就缺席，可能会让人们自行猜测您缺席的原因——他们最有可能想到的原因之一就是他们对您来说不够重要。在他们最脆弱的时候，这种想法会让他们非常难过。玛丽安说，看到那么多人抽出时间来悼念她的母亲，让她深受感动。

Some situations make people feel so uncomfortable that they just can’t face going to the funeral home or funeral. Seeing the dead body of someone you once knew can be frightening, or it might trigger a memory involving a loved one who passed. Granted, going to a funeral home is not the most pleasant experience, but you should not let that stop you from showing up. If you do not want to visit the body, don’t. You can still pay your respects and pass along fond memories to loved ones without ever having to approach the casket. In fact, oftentimes, there isn’t a body to be viewed at the funeral home. Videos and pictures might be displayed that will trigger great memories of the deceased or help you to remember a story to share with their family members.

有些情况会让人感到非常不自在，以至于无法面对去殡仪馆或参加葬礼。看到曾经认识的人的遗体可能会令人恐惧，或者勾起与逝去亲人相关的回忆。诚然，去殡仪馆并非愉快的经历，但这不应该成为你拒绝前往的理由。如果你不想瞻仰遗容，那就不要去。你仍然可以表达敬意，并将美好的回忆传递给亲人，而无需靠近棺材。事实上，很多时候，殡仪馆里并没有遗体供人瞻仰。那里可能会播放一些视频和照片，这些内容会唤起你对逝者的美好回忆，或者帮助你想起一些可以与家人分享的故事。

Those left behind need your support. Be willing to endure a few hours of your own discomfort in order to provide comfort to the grieving. If you don’t, you could so hurt someone you love that you will lose a valued relationship with them. People don’t forget who was “not there” for them when they needed them the most.

那些留下来的人需要你的支持。请你愿意忍受几个小时的不适，去安慰那些悲痛欲绝的人。如果你不这样做，你可能会深深伤害你爱的人，甚至失去一段珍贵的关系。人们不会忘记在他们最需要帮助的时候，谁“不在身边”。

Ignoring Loss Based on Your Own Value Judgments

基于自身价值判断而忽视损失

Families come in all shapes and sizes these days. Couples today are made up of partners, companions, and spouses. Families may include people of all races, sexes, and creeds. There is no one way to describe a family other than as a group of people who live together and love one another. And when someone in your family dies, be it a traditional or nontraditional family, it hurts just the same.

如今，家庭的形式多种多样。伴侣可以是夫妻，也可以是配偶。家庭成员可能来自不同的种族、性别和信仰。家庭的定义无非就是一群生活在一起、彼此相爱的人。无论你的家庭是传统家庭还是非传统家庭，当有亲人离世时，那种痛苦都是一样的。

Misty explains that it was very hurtful when people did not even acknowledge the loss of the man she considered her stepfather in what she describes as her nontraditional family. “Because my mom was not married to this man and he was not my official stepfather, some people did not acknowledge our grief. This was devastating. This man was as much a part of our family as my blood relatives,” she said. “Love is love, regardless of blood relations or marital status.”

米斯蒂解释说，在她这个非传统家庭中，人们甚至不去关注她视为继父的那个人的离世，这让她非常伤心。“因为我妈妈没有嫁给这个人，他也不是我正式的继父，所以有些人不理解我们的悲痛。这太让人崩溃了。这个人就像我的血亲一样，是我们家庭的一份子，”她说。“爱就是爱，无关血缘关系，也无关婚姻状况。”

Making Comments about Funeral Arrangements (or the Lack Thereof)

对葬礼安排（或缺乏葬礼安排）发表评论

These days, people make different choices when it comes to funeral arrangements. So you might want to think twice before you start whispering about whether it was proper that the casket was open or closed, that the service was held in a church or a funeral parlor, or that there was no service at all. Many people arrange their funeral years in advance, and what you see could be exactly what that the deceased wanted or what makes the process of grieving easiest for the family. Remember, too, that many of these decisions come at a time when it is difficult to think things through, so some details may fall through the cracks. There may even be differences in opinions or family drama surrounding funerals.

如今，人们在安排葬礼方面会做出不同的选择。因此，在你开始私下议论棺材是打开还是关闭、葬礼是在教堂还是殡仪馆举行，或者干脆不举行葬礼是否合适之前，或许应该三思。许多人会提前数年安排好自己的葬礼，而你所看到的可能正是逝者生前的意愿，或者能够最大程度地减轻家属悲痛的因素。此外，请记住，许多此类决定都是在人们难以冷静思考的时候做出的，因此一些细节可能会被忽略。甚至在葬礼安排方面，也可能存在意见分歧或家庭矛盾。

It is not your place to get involved or even comment on what you think is proper. Besides, is this really the right time to be critical? When grieving friends and relatives are at their most vulnerable, offer your support and hold back on the criticism.

你没有资格插手此事，甚至不应该对你认为合适的做法指手画脚。再说，现在真的是批评的合适时机吗？当亲朋好友悲痛欲绝、最脆弱的时候，你应该给予支持，而不是批评指责。

Being Too Quick to Change the Subject

太快转移话题

Many people feel that if they change the subject when their friend or relative starts talking about death, it will help lessen their pain. How many times have you heard or even said, “Oh, please don’t cry because if you start crying, then I’ll start crying!”?

许多人觉得，当朋友或亲人开始谈论死亡时，如果转移话题，就能减轻他们的痛苦。你是不是也经常听到甚至说过这样的话：“哦，别哭，你要是哭了，我也会跟着哭！”？

Healing comes from talking, venting, remembering, and crying. This is all a part of healthy healing. A strong, willing shoulder to cry on is often needed most. No answer is necessary, nor is one expected. So let them talk, vent, and cry; it will be the fastest way for them to feel better. Remember, people can only get through their grief—they can’t go above, under, or around it. So allow it to happen by providing a willing ear.

疗愈源于倾诉、发泄、回忆和哭泣。这些都是健康疗愈过程的一部分。他们最需要的往往是一个可以依靠的、愿意倾听的肩膀。不需要答案，也不期待答案。所以，让他们倾诉、发泄、哭泣；这将是他们感觉好转最快的方式。记住，人们只能经历悲伤——他们无法凌驾于悲伤之上，也无法逃避或绕过它。所以，请敞开心扉，倾听他们的心声。

Giving Your Opinion of the Cause of Death

发表您对死因的看法

People always seem to be extremely curious about what caused someone to die. Sometimes the reason is obvious: cancer, heart attack, car accident, etc. But sometimes the family may not want to share that information. If that’s the case, leave it be. It is really none of your business, and playing detective to try and figure it out is not only rude, but very hurtful. Perhaps the deceased perished in a way that would have been embarrassing; perhaps they had a disease they didn’t want anyone to know about—or perhaps they had a mental illness, which, unfortunately, is still stigmatized in this country.

人们似乎总是对死因充满好奇。有时死因显而易见：癌症、心脏病、车祸等等。但有时家属可能不愿透露这些信息。如果真是如此，那就别管了。这真的与你无关，试图像侦探一样去探寻真相不仅无礼，而且非常伤人。或许逝者的死因令人难堪；或许他们患有不愿为人知的疾病——又或许他们患有精神疾病，不幸的是，精神疾病在这个国家仍然饱受歧视。

One of the most devastating deaths to understand is suicide. The thought that someone is so troubled that they see the end of their life as the only solution seems unimaginable to so many of us. Yet people take their own lives daily and leave behind loved ones who may be riddled with guilt and shame and who may have questions that may never be answered.

最令人痛心的死亡之一莫过于自杀。对我们许多人来说，难以想象有人会因为如此痛苦而将结束生命视为唯一的出路。然而，每天都有人结束自己的生命，留下的亲人可能充满愧疚和羞耻，心中充满永远无法解答的疑问。

Charmaine suffered tremendously following her father’s suicide. Not only did she have to deal with the loss of her father, but she had to deal with other people’s judgment. Most hurtful was when people told her that her father was now in hell. She said she is grateful that her priest was comforting and not judgmental at this extremely difficult time.

父亲自杀后，夏尔曼遭受了巨大的痛苦。她不仅要承受失去父亲的悲痛，还要面对外界的评判。最让她难过的是，有人说她父亲现在下了地狱。她说，她很感激她的牧师在她最艰难的时刻给予她安慰，而不是评判她。

Being Impatient with the Person’s Grieving Process

对他人哀悼过程缺乏耐心

Although there are stages of grief, there is no timeline for being back to one’s “normal” self. In fact, many experience a “new normal” after the death of a loved one. Some people may be able to heal rather quickly; others can take years to heal. So don’t expect someone to be “over it” just because you think enough time has passed.

虽然悲伤会经历不同的阶段，但恢复到“正常”状态并没有固定的时间表。事实上，许多人在失去挚爱后都会经历一种“新的常态”。有些人可能很快就能走出悲伤，而有些人则需要数年时间。所以，不要因为你觉得时间已经过去很久就期望别人“走出了阴影”。

Sometimes, someone suffering from a loss will seem to be doing really well and will then have a setback. It can happen, and they should not feel weak or inadequate because of it. Each person has their own way to grieve.

有时候，遭受丧亲之痛的人表面上看起来状态很好，但之后可能会遭遇挫折。这种情况时有发生，他们不应该因此感到软弱或无能。每个人都有自己独特的悲伤方式。

Also, don’t compare them to other people you know and how they dealt with loss. Every situation and every person is different; your job is to help them heal at their own pace.

此外，不要拿他们和您认识的其他人比较，也不要比较他们是如何应对失去亲人的。每个人的情况都不同，您的职责是帮助他们按照自己的节奏疗愈。

Critiquing Their Grieving Process

批判他们的哀悼过程

People grieve in so many different ways. Some cry inconsolably; some shut down emotionally; some might even feel a certain sense of relief, especially if they have seen their loved one suffer through a long illness.

人们表达悲伤的方式多种多样。有些人会痛哭流涕；有些人会封闭自己的情感；有些人甚至可能会感到某种解脱，尤其是在他们亲眼目睹所爱之人长期饱受疾病折磨之后。

Mary lost her husband following a long bout of suffering. She shared the following: “A relative said I showed no emotion. My husband had been sick so long that I had been through all the emotions, anger, and breakdowns. I now knew he was at peace and pain free. He would not have wanted me to be like that. The last week of his illness was my hardest, but a sense of comfort knowing he was not in pain was good for me.”

玛丽的丈夫在与病魔抗争多年后去世了。她分享道：“一位亲戚说我毫无表情。丈夫病了这么久，我已经经历了各种各样的情绪，愤怒、崩溃，都曾有过。现在我知道他安息了，不再受苦了。他肯定不希望我这样。他病重的最后一周是我最难熬的，但知道他不再受苦，这让我感到一丝安慰。”

Not-So-Helpful Words

不太有用的词语

The following phrases have also been found to be more hurtful than helpful:

经研究发现，以下这些短语弊大于利：

“If you need anything, just let me know.”

“如果你需要什么，尽管告诉我。”

For the most part, these words are uttered with the best intentions. You would do anything if you just knew what to do. But the problem is, most grieving people simply don’t know what they need at this time, and they are not likely to pick up the phone and ask for help. Instead of asking them what they need, perhaps it would be better to offer them suggestions as to how you might help, such as running errands, doing laundry, or driving them somewhere.

大多数情况下，这些话都是出于好意。如果你知道该怎么做，你什么都愿意做。但问题是，大多数悲伤的人根本不知道自己此刻需要什么，他们也不太可能拿起电话寻求帮助。与其问他们需要什么，或许不如主动提出一些你可以提供的帮助建议，比如跑腿办事、洗衣服或者开车送他们去某个地方。

“Your loved one is better off now.”

“你的亲人现在情况好多了。”

People who are mourning the loss of someone they love usually believe “better off” would be with them, not dead. One of the survey respondents, Michelle, lost her father in a plane accident. Many people told her dying that way was better because it was quick. Others said he died doing what he loved. She says the pain of loss is the same whether the death is sudden and traumatic or whether it follows a long illness. Even though he did die doing something he loved, Michelle hates the thought that her father was going down in a plane and perhaps fully aware that the end was just seconds away.

那些正在哀悼亲人离世的人通常认为，逝者“最好”是留在身边，而不是死去。受访者之一米歇尔的父亲在一场空难中丧生。许多人告诉她，那样死去更好，因为死得快。也有人说，他是在做自己热爱的事情时去世的。米歇尔说，无论死亡是突如其来的、令人痛苦的，还是在长期患病后去世，失去亲人的痛苦都是一样的。尽管父亲确实是在做自己热爱的事情时去世的，但米歇尔仍然无法接受父亲当时乘坐的飞机坠毁，而且可能完全清楚死亡就在眼前。

“I know what you’re going through.”

“我知道你正在经历什么。”

You don’t. All situations are different. After Judy’s husband died unexpectedly, she had a hard time with people saying, “I know how you feel.” She said, “No, they don’t know how I feel. Death—like birth, marriage, and raising children—is individual. Many of us experience the same things, but we all do it in our individual ways and our feelings are our own.” You may have experienced something similar, but you don’t know how someone else feels.

你不知道。每个人的情况都不一样。朱迪的丈夫意外去世后，人们总是对她说“我懂你的感受”，这让她很难接受。她说：“不，他们不懂我的感受。死亡——就像出生、结婚和养育子女一样——是因人而异的。我们很多人经历着同样的事情，但我们处理的方式各不相同，我们的感受也只有我们自己知道。”你或许经历过类似的事情，但你无法真正了解别人的感受。

“You don’t look upset,” or “Why are you showing no emotion?”

“你看上去并不难过，”或者“你为什么没有表现出任何情绪？”

Remember, as we said in the previous section on hurtful actions, everyone is different and the way they grieve is just as different. Don’t project your mental picture of what you think is the correct or best way to grieve onto someone else.

记住，正如我们在上一节关于伤害性行为的论述中所说，每个人都是不同的，他们悲伤的方式也各不相同。不要把你认为正确或最好的悲伤方式强加给别人。

For instance, if someone has cared for a loved one during a long illness, their grieving process and emotions have been flowing for quite some time. They may actually feel a sense of relief along with their sadness—and there is nothing wrong with them feeling that way. Until you have walked a mile in their shoes, do not judge how they are dealing with their loss.

例如，如果有人长期照顾患病的亲人，他们的悲伤和情绪可能已经持续了相当长的时间。他们或许会在悲伤之余感到一丝解脱——这种感觉完全可以理解。除非你真正感同身受，否则不要妄加评判他们如何应对失去亲人的痛苦。

3. Supporting Those with Terminal or Chronic Illness/Disability

3. 为身患绝症或慢性疾病/残疾的人士提供支持

When a loved one or a family member is terminally or chronically ill, there are many things you can do to really help them out. It’s very important you show that you care for them, especially because this is often a time when many so-called friends disappear. There are several things you can do, including talking about your loved one’s illness, getting community involved, understanding the “rules” of visiting, learning how to care for the caregiver, and knowing what things to avoid when a loved one is terminally or chronically ill. This chapter talks about each of these ways to support your loved one.

当您挚爱的亲人或家人身患绝症或慢性病时，您可以做很多事情来真正帮助他们。表达您的关心至关重要，尤其是在这个时期，许多所谓的“朋友”往往会消失。您可以做很多事情，包括与亲人谈论他们的病情、让社区参与进来、了解探视的“规则”、学习如何照顾照护者，以及了解在亲人身患绝症或慢性病时应该避免哪些事情。本章将逐一探讨这些支持您挚爱亲人的方法。

Don’t Be Afraid to Talk!

不要害怕说话！

First and foremost, maintain communication with a friend who’s ill. Sometimes, a terminal illness like cancer scares people away. It’s as if it’s contagious. Some flee because it is simply too painful to face the sick person or the possibility that someone near and dear is dying. Some simply don’t want to talk about it, or the situation makes them feel uncomfortable. Some may think to themselves, I’ll call tomorrow —but they never do. Whatever the reason, the phone calls and visits just stop. If any of these excuses cross your mind, remember: You can’t catch cancer, mental illness, or most other ailments. You’ll handle the situation much better if you focus on their pain and what they are facing, rather than your feelings about it.

首先，务必与患病的朋友保持联系。有时，像癌症这样的绝症会吓跑一些人，仿佛它会传染。有些人逃避，是因为面对病人或亲人即将离世的现实实在太痛苦。有些人只是不想谈论这件事，或者觉得这种处境让他们感到不自在。有些人可能会想：“明天再打电话吧”，但他们最终却什么也没做。无论原因是什么，电话和探望都戛然而止。如果你脑海中浮现出这些借口，请记住：癌症、精神疾病或大多数其他疾病都不会传染。与其关注自己的感受，不如把注意力放在他们的痛苦和他们正在经历的事情上，这样你才能更好地应对这种情况。

It’s OK not to know what to say. Be honest. Tell them, “I don’t know what to say, but I’m here for you.” Often, people don’t even want to talk about their situation—they just crave normal conversation. But if they do want to talk, be there to listen. Listen without interruption and without judgment. You may find that you don’t have to say anything at all.

不知道该说什么也没关系。坦诚一点。告诉他们：“我不知道该说什么，但我会一直陪着你。”很多时候，人们甚至不想谈论自己的处境——他们只是渴望正常的对话。但如果他们真的想倾诉，那就静静地倾听。不要打断，也不要评判。你或许会发现，你根本不需要说任何话。

No one wants to be alone. Remember the AIDS epidemic? Many men suffered and died alone simply because they were stigmatized for having the “gay disease.” Many had family and friends simply desert them, and others were too ashamed to ask for help.

没有人愿意孤独终老。还记得艾滋病疫情吗？许多男性仅仅因为被贴上“同性恋疾病”的标签而遭受痛苦，最终孤独地死去。许多人的家人和朋友都离他们而去，还有一些人羞于启齿，不敢寻求帮助。

Diane, who suffers from chronic bipolar disorder, says, “A hug is always nice when you don’t know what to say. The majority of people don’t like feeling alone.” Talking about her illness is especially important to Diane. “I would rather they ask me about it than assume they know me,” she says. “I know some people think bipolar people go out and kill people or that we’re all crazy. But just like any other disease, if we find the right medication combination, we can function normally.”

患有慢性双相情感障碍的黛安说：“当你不知道该说什么的时候，一个拥抱总是很温暖的。大多数人都不喜欢孤独的感觉。” 谈论自己的病情对黛安来说尤为重要。“我宁愿他们主动询问，而不是想当然地认为他们了解我，”她说。“我知道有些人认为双相情感障碍患者会出去杀人，或者认为我们都是疯子。但就像其他疾病一样，如果我们找到合适的药物组合，我们也能正常生活。”

Doug, a cancer survivor, adds that he actually wished he had been asked “How do you feel?” more often while undergoing treatment. He said that would have allowed him to really talk about what he was facing. He did use Facebook to keep people updated on his treatments and procedures, and he said that social media allowed people he hadn’t heard from in years to contact him.

癌症幸存者道格补充说，他其实希望在治疗期间有人能更频繁地问他“感觉怎么样？”。他说，那样他就能真正谈谈自己正在经历的一切。他确实会利用Facebook来更新自己的治疗和手术进展，他还说，社交媒体让多年未联系的人也能联系上他。

A person with a terrible illness or disability is still the same person inside; it’s just the conditions of their life that have changed. They may not be able to do the things they used to do, but they are still able to enjoy the love and connection of a dear friend. So don’t let the fact that your friend can’t bowl every Friday night anymore stop you from interacting with them. How about playing a game of cards at home or renting a movie to watch together instead?

身患重病或残疾的人，其内心依然是原来的自己；改变的只是生活环境。他们或许无法再像以前那样做很多事情，但依然能够享受挚友的爱与陪伴。所以，不要因为你的朋友不能再像以前那样每周五晚上一起打保龄球而停止与他们交流。不如一起在家玩玩牌，或者租部电影来看吧？

Granted, hearing of a painful, possibly terminal diagnosis may evoke fear and sadness in you and may make you reluctant to see a person whom you admire, respect, and love looking so sick, frail, and vulnerable. But it’s vital that you summon the strength to fight those feelings. So if you can, resume the same conversations you had before the diagnosis. In fact, many people say they just want to be treated normally again.

诚然，听到令人痛苦、甚至可能是绝症的诊断结果，可能会让你感到恐惧和悲伤，让你不愿看到自己敬佩、尊重和爱的人如此病弱、虚弱和无助。但至关重要的是，你要鼓起勇气克服这些负面情绪。所以，如果可以的话，尽量恢复到确诊前你们的对话。事实上，许多人都表示，他们只是希望再次被正常对待。

That’s exactly what friends did for Beth. A breast cancer survivor who is very active in her community, Beth says that one of the best things anyone did was tell her, “I still need you to chair this committee.” She says she needed that project to keep her focused on the future. Distraction is also a great healer according to Barb. Knowing that she was artistic, a friend sent her a simple coloring book and crayons after she had her leg amputated. This thoughtful present was a great mood booster for her. “Each day for an hour I would color a page. After I was done with the coloring book, I sent it back to my friend and told him it was the best thing he could have done for me.”

朋友们正是这样帮助贝丝的。贝丝是一位乳腺癌幸存者，积极参与社区活动。她说，朋友们对她说的最好的一句话就是：“我仍然需要你担任这个委员会的主席。” 她说，她需要这个项目来让她专注于未来。芭芭拉也认为，转移注意力也是一种很好的疗愈方式。一位朋友知道她有艺术细胞，在她截肢后送给她一本简单的涂色书和蜡笔。这份贴心的礼物极大地改善了她的心情。“我每天花一个小时涂一页。涂完后，我把涂色书寄还给了朋友，告诉他这是他能为我做的最好的事。”

If you happen to run into someone you haven’t seen in a while and they look visibly changed by a disability or illness, don’t hesitate to ask them about what happened or how they are feeling. Most of the people I interviewed said they are OK talking about their situation, as long as you come from a place of genuine care and concern versus appearing to be prying into their personal life out of morbid curiosity.

如果你碰巧遇到一位许久未见的朋友，而他/她因残疾或疾病而容貌明显改变，请不要犹豫，主动询问他/她发生了什么事，或者感觉如何。我采访过的大多数人都表示，只要你是出于真诚的关心和关注，而不是出于病态的好奇心窥探他们的隐私，他们并不介意谈论自己的情况。

Trisha suffers from many chronic disorders and doesn’t mind at all that people inquire about her illnesses. In fact, she encourages people to ask questions. She says, “I would much rather they have accurate information that originated from me than wrong information that is repeated and perpetuated through rumor or guesswork.”

特里莎患有多种慢性疾病，但她一点也不介意别人询问她的病情。事实上，她鼓励人们提问。她说：“我宁愿他们获得来自我本人的准确信息，也不愿他们听到那些通过谣言或猜测而传播的错误信息。”

Having walked with crutches for most of his life due to cerebral palsy, Dale says he would much rather people ask him about his disability than stare at him. He says, “It makes me feel like a circus freak. I want them to know I am really no different. I laugh, cry, eat, poop, and have a twisted sense of humor too. I may be trapped in a body that doesn’t look like yours, but I have always tried to be an inspiration to other people and to let them know, ‘It’s OK. You can ask. I won’t bite unless you want me to.’”

由于患有脑瘫，戴尔一生大部分时间都拄着拐杖行走。他说，他宁愿人们询问他的残疾情况，而不是盯着他看。他说：“那样会让我觉得自己像个马戏团的怪胎。我希望他们知道，我其实和别人没什么两样。我也会笑，会哭，会吃饭，会拉屎，而且我也有自己独特的幽默感。我可能被困在一个和别人不一样的身体里，但我一直努力激励他人，让他们知道：‘没关系，你可以问。除非你想让我问，否则我不会咬人。’”

James also has cerebral palsy. He doesn’t mind talking to people about it, as long as it is in an intimate, respectful way. For him, asking about his illness one-on-one is fine, but it’s not OK when there are a lot of other people around. That kind of attention to his disability makes him uncomfortable.

詹姆斯也患有脑瘫。他不介意和别人谈论这件事，只要谈话方式亲切且尊重他。对他来说，一对一地询问他的病情没问题，但如果周围有很多其他人，这就不合适了。那种对他残疾的过度关注会让他感到不自在。

Strength in Numbers

人多力量大

When a loved one or friend is sick or in need, don’t feel you have to provide help all by yourself. Communities, too, can come together to help the sick and their caregivers.

当你的亲人或朋友生病或需要帮助时，不要觉得你必须独自提供帮助。社区也可以团结起来，帮助病人及其照护者。

When Debbie’s daughter, Erin, was in the hospital with cervical cancer, the outpouring of love and support she and her family received was so appreciated. “Our community held a spaghetti dinner fundraiser for Erin.” In addition to the fundraiser, Debbie said her entire church family prayed for them, made a prayer blanket for Erin, and even supplied a gas card to help pay the expenses the family incurred traveling to and from the hospital.

黛比的女儿艾琳因宫颈癌住院期间，她和家人收到了来自各方的爱与支持，这让他们无比感激。“我们社区为艾琳举办了一场意大利面晚餐筹款活动。”黛比说，除了筹款活动，她整个教会的弟兄姐妹都为他们祈祷，为艾琳制作了一条祈祷毯，甚至还提供了一张加油卡，帮助他们支付往返医院的交通费用。

Many times, friends and neighbors will set up schedules for sending over meals for a family that is dealing with an illness. They might also pitch in to care for children or perform chores when the chaos of dealing with this kind of situation strikes.

很多时候，朋友和邻居会安排时间，定期为生病的家庭送餐。当这种情况变得混乱不堪时，他们也可能会帮忙照顾孩子或做些家务。

Those surveyed said they felt comfort, support, and love when their community of friends reached out. Having others take the time to care about them, their needs, and the needs of their family at a time when it mattered most had a definite impact on everyone involved.

受访者表示，当朋友们伸出援手时，他们感到安慰、支持和关爱。在最关键的时刻，有人抽出时间关心他们、他们的需求以及他们家人的需求，这对所有相关人员都产生了切实的影响。

Understanding the “Rules” of Visiting

了解参观“规则”

When it comes to visiting someone who is ill, whether at home or at a hospital or care center, it’s probably best to check with a family member to find out if visiting is allowed and if the person is up to having visitors. For some people, having someone to talk to and laugh with is the perfect medicine and a great way to pass the time and forget about their illness for a while. Others, however, may not want anyone to see them in such a fragile state. Ask rather than assume that you know what your friend prefers. If the person is up for a visit, then make the time to go. You likely won’t be spending a lot of time there, but showing up can mean a lot.

探望生病的人，无论是在家、医院还是护理中心，最好先询问家人是否允许探视，以及病人是否愿意接待访客。对一些人来说，有人陪伴聊天、欢笑是最好的良药，也是消磨时间、暂时忘却病痛的好方法。然而，有些人可能不希望任何人看到自己如此虚弱的一面。与其想当然地认为自己知道朋友的意愿，不如事先询问。如果病人愿意接受探望，那就抽出时间去看看。你可能不会在那里待很久，但你的出现意义非凡。

Visiting someone who is ill or dying can be very daunting. Illness takes its toll on the body, and some will look very different as the disease progresses. They may have lost weight, lost their hair, or even lost some of their memory; it may be very unsettling to see them. Add to that all the round-the-clock care, machines, tubes, and other distractions that may be present, and it can be most unpleasant.

探望病人或临终之人往往令人倍感煎熬。疾病会侵蚀身体，随着病情发展，有些人的外貌会发生显著变化。他们可能体重下降、头发脱落，甚至记忆力衰退；看到他们的样子，难免会让人感到不安。再加上全天候的护理、各种仪器、管线以及其他可能分散注意力的因素，探望的过程就更加令人难受了。

These are the kinds of things that keep many people away. It can all be boiled down to one word: fear. However, this is a time when you have to remind yourself that the cost of NOT going could be much higher than that of gathering your courage and showing up. There are really not many people who love to visit the sick in the hospital, and there are those who are uncomfortable or feel awkward about it. But remember, this is not about you; it is about your friend or loved one, so visit despite these feelings.

正是这些原因让很多人望而却步。归根结底，可以用一个词来概括：恐惧。然而，此时此刻，你必须提醒自己，不去探望的代价可能远高于鼓起勇气前往探望的代价。的确，很少有人喜欢去医院探望病人，有些人甚至会感到不自在或尴尬。但请记住，这不是为了你自己，而是为了你的朋友或爱人，所以，即使有这些感受，也要去探望。

I’ll never forget that when my son was in the neonatal intensive care unit, a friend of mine who was terrified of hospitals drove over an hour to visit my husband and me and to see our son. Knowing how difficult this was for her, the fact that she “sucked it up” and came anyway meant more to me than anything else she could have done. Her willingness to push past her own fears when we needed her support did the most to show me how much she loved and cared about us.

我永远不会忘记，当我的儿子在新生儿重症监护室时，我的一位朋友，一个非常害怕医院的人，驱车一个多小时来看望我和我丈夫，也来看望我们的儿子。我知道这对她来说有多么艰难，但她还是“克服恐惧”前来探望，这比她能做的任何事情都更让我感动。在我们最需要她支持的时候，她愿意克服自己的恐惧，这让我最能感受到她对我们的爱和关心。

Caring for the Caregivers

关爱照护者

Terminal illness takes its toll on caregivers too. When dealing with the illness of a loved one, the stress can be overwhelming. Many caregivers are forced to give up their jobs to care full time for someone at home, while others may be trying to hold down a job and still be there to help with the care after work.

绝症也会给照护者带来沉重的负担。面对亲人的疾病，压力往往令人难以承受。许多照护者被迫放弃工作，全职在家照顾病人，而另一些人则需要在工作之余，抽出时间在下班后继续提供照护。

A lot of caregivers are deprived. They’re deprived of sleep, nourishment, self-care, and friends—basically deprived of life as they once knew it. Their whole world now centers on the one who is sick. They’re afraid of losing someone they love. They also face the frustration of being unable to lessen their loved one’s pain or stop the progression of the disease, illness, or disability.

许多照护者都身心俱疲。他们睡眠不足、营养匮乏、无法照顾自己、也缺乏朋友——基本上失去了他们曾经熟悉的生活。他们的整个世界现在都围绕着病人转。他们害怕失去所爱之人。他们也面临着无法减轻亲人痛苦或阻止疾病、残疾或残疾恶化的挫败感。

Oftentimes, caregivers need a break from their duties but hesitate to ask anyone to step in. Many may also need to vent their fear, frustration, and sadness, as well as their guilt for even having those feelings. Some believe they are not entitled to feel that way because, after all, they are not the one suffering from an illness or disability. But they too are suffering.

很多时候，照顾者需要休息，却又不好意思开口求助。许多人可能还需要倾诉内心的恐惧、沮丧和悲伤，以及因为有这些情绪而产生的内疚感。有些人认为自己没有资格有这些感受，毕竟，他们不是患病或残疾的人。但他们也在承受痛苦。

Here are some things you can do that might make life just a little easier for those who are sick or caring for the sick:

以下这些事情或许能让生病的人或照顾病人的人的生活轻松一些：

• Send a meal. A home-cooked meal is always a welcome treat. Someone who is ill or caring for someone who is ill might not have a lot of time to plan meals or cook. If you find yourself limited on time, you can still provide food by ordering takeout and delivering it or having it delivered. You could also send gift cards for pizza or takeout, allowing them to choose what they want to eat and when they want it.

• 送餐。一顿家常饭菜总是令人欣喜的。生病的人或照顾病人的人可能没有太多时间计划或烹饪。如果您时间有限，仍然可以通过订购外卖并送餐的方式提供食物。您也可以赠送披萨或外卖的礼品卡，让他们自己选择想吃什么以及什么时候吃。

• Send gas cards. Trips to and from the doctor’s office or hospital and to out-of-town medical facilities can add up. You can help ease the financial burden a bit by filling up the gas tank.

• 赠送加油卡。往返诊所、医院以及前往外地医疗机构的路费加起来可能不菲。您可以帮他们加满油箱，从而减轻一些经济负担。

• Send grocery cards. Costs involved in caring for yourself or someone else with a terminal or chronic illness can become overwhelming. Sometimes, doctors prescribe special diets that include foods that are natural, organic, and fresh, which are often more expensive. Providing a grocery card for a store that offers these kinds of foods is especially thoughtful. Grocery cards can be used personally by the sick or by those caring for a loved one at home or at an extended-stay facility.

• 赠送食品杂货卡。照顾自己或身患绝症或慢性病的亲人，其费用可能令人难以承受。有时，医生会开具特殊的饮食方案，其中包含天然、有机和新鲜的食物，这些食物通常价格更高。赠送一张提供此类食品的超市的食品杂货卡尤其贴心。食品杂货卡既可供病人本人使用，也可供在家或在长期护理机构照顾亲人的家属使用。

• Run errands. Being sick or caring for the sick can be exhausting, but life goes on. Banking, grocery shopping, and picking up prescriptions—these things still need to be done. You can really help someone by offering to do something they are probably just too busy or too tired to do.

• 帮忙跑腿。生病或照顾病人会让人筋疲力尽，但生活还要继续。去银行、买菜、取药——这些事情仍然需要做。如果你主动提出帮别人做他们可能太忙或太累而无暇顾及的事情，就能真正帮到他们。

• Babysit. An offer to babysit while your friend goes for treatment can be a godsend, but an offer to watch the kids just so your friend can have an hour or two of peace and quiet can be just as valuable. If the children are older, you may want to invite them out for an activity like spending an afternoon at the park or going to a movie. The children will have fun and escape the tension at home, and the patient or caregiver will have some much needed rest.

• 帮忙照看孩子。朋友接受治疗期间，主动提出帮忙照看孩子无疑是雪中送炭；即使只是帮忙照看孩子，让朋友能享受一两个小时的宁静，也同样意义非凡。如果孩子年龄稍大，不妨邀请他们外出活动，比如去公园玩一下午或者去看场电影。孩子们可以玩得开心，暂时摆脱家里的紧张气氛，而病人或照护者也能得到急需的休息。

• Offer to transport kids to events or school. Someone who is sick may be too tired to keep up with the many activities that keep their children busy. Those with weakened immune systems often can’t go out in public. Offering to be a “taxi driver” can be a big help.

• 主动提出接送孩子参加活动或上学。生病的人可能太过疲惫，无法跟上孩子们的各种活动。免疫系统较弱的人往往无法外出。主动承担“司机”的角色可以帮上很大的忙。

• Offer caregivers a break. Caring for an ill loved one can be a round-the-clock job with very few breaks for sleep, let alone anything else in life. An offer to take over the caregiving duties for a few hours will give that caregiver an opportunity to either rest or take some much needed time for himself.

• 让照护者休息一下。照顾生病的亲人可能是一项全天候的工作，几乎没有休息时间，更别提其他生活琐事了。主动提出帮忙照护几个小时，就能让照护者有机会休息或抽出一些宝贵的个人时间。

• Offer to clean the house or mow the lawn. Scrubbing floors and bathrooms takes time, effort, and energy—all of which are in short supply for anyone who is sick. An offer to do light cleaning might be appreciated. Maintaining the lawn may also be a chore that simply is too strenuous for someone dealing with illness. Offering to mow the grass or to pay to have a service do it may be a great blessing.

• 主动提出打扫房屋或修剪草坪。擦洗地板和浴室需要时间、精力和体力——而这些对于任何生病的人来说都是极其匮乏的。主动提出做一些简单的清洁工作，他们可能会非常感激。维护草坪对于生病的人来说也可能是一项过于繁重的家务。主动提出修剪草坪或支付费用请人修剪草坪，对他们来说无疑是莫大的帮助。

• Call. Make a phone call and ask if the person is up to talking. If they are, take the time to listen to what they want to tell you. They may want to talk about what they are going through, or they may want to talk about anything other than their illness. Just let them know you called because you care, not because you wanted the latest on their diagnosis. Even if they say they don’t feel like talking, knowing you are thinking of them is a real spirit booster.

• 打电话。打个电话问问对方是否愿意说话。如果他们愿意，请花时间认真倾听他们想说的话。他们可能想谈谈自己的经历，也可能想聊聊疾病以外的任何事情。让他们知道你打电话是因为你关心他们，而不是为了打听他们的最新诊断结果。即使他们说不想说话，知道你在惦记着他们，也会让他们感到莫大的安慰。

• Send cards. Just about everyone loves a note of encouragement or a card that lets them know they are in your thoughts. Sending a card is a great way to show you care.

• 寄送贺卡。几乎每个人都喜欢收到鼓励的话语或贺卡，让他们知道你在惦记着他们。寄送贺卡是表达关心的一种绝佳方式。

• Drive. Sometimes, medications make it impossible for someone to drive; others may simply be too sick. An offer to drive your friend to church, the doctor’s office, or the mall is very helpful.

• 开车。有时，药物会使人无法开车；有些人可能只是病得太重。主动提出开车送朋友去教堂、诊所或商场是非常有帮助的。

• Take care of pets. Pet care is a real concern for anyone who is currently disabled or facing time in a hospital or other care facility. If it is possible for you to check in on the pets daily or even house them until your friend is back on his feet again, it can really give your friend peace of mind.

• 照顾宠物。对于目前行动不便或需要在医院或其他护理机构接受治疗的人来说，宠物照料是他们非常关心的问题。如果您能每天探望宠物，甚至在您的朋友康复之前暂时照看它们，这将大大减轻他/她的负担。

• Keep in touch via the Internet. Many folks dealing with illnesses have weakened immune systems and can’t go out in public or even have visitors. The Internet and social networking sites are great ways to keep in touch, challenge each other to games, and even have live visual conversations via a service such as Skype.

• 通过网络保持联系。许多患病的人免疫系统较弱，无法外出，甚至无法接待访客。互联网和社交网站是保持联系、互相挑战游戏，甚至通过 Skype 等服务进行实时视频通话的绝佳途径。

• Pray. You can offer prayers at your own discretion, place the person on a prayer list at your church, or offer to pray with the person. Many people take great comfort in knowing someone cares for them so much that they will pray for them or with them.

• 祷告。您可以根据自己的意愿为对方祷告，也可以将对方列入教会的祷告名单，或者主动提出与对方一同祷告。许多人会因为知道有人如此关心自己，愿意为他们祷告或与他们一同祷告而感到莫大的安慰。

• Watch a funny movie. Experts agree that laughter helps people heal faster and feel better. Offer to take someone to a movie or just bring one of their favorite comedies over and watch it with them. Laughter really is great medicine.

• 看部喜剧电影。专家一致认为，笑声有助于人们更快康复，感觉更好。不妨主动邀请朋友去看电影，或者带上他们最喜欢的喜剧片，一起观看。笑声真的是一剂良药。

• Send a salon or spa certificate. A soothing massage can melt away tension and stress in someone who is sick or caring for a sick loved one. This gift of human touch can also be very healing. A pedicure is another nice way to allow someone to relax and be cared for. Many salons and spas offer gift certificates for these kinds of services.

• 送一张美容院或水疗中心的礼券。舒缓的按摩可以消除病人或照顾病人的紧张和压力。这种人与人之间的接触也能起到很好的疗愈作用。足疗也是让对方放松身心、享受呵护的好方法。许多美容院和水疗中心都提供这类服务的礼券。

• Gather friends to plan an event. Gather friends or coworkers to plan a fundraiser to assist with medical bills or travel costs. Community events like this raise money, awareness, and the spirits not only of those who are participating, but also of the person or people who are the beneficiaries of such acts of care and kindness. When everyone works together, great things can happen and no one person bears all the responsibility.

• 召集朋友一起策划活动。召集朋友或同事策划筹款活动，以帮助支付医疗费用或差旅费。这类社区活动不仅能筹集资金、提高公众意识，还能鼓舞参与者的精神，更能帮助那些受益于这些关爱和善举的个人或群体。当大家齐心协力时，就能成就伟业，而且没有人需要独自承担所有责任。

What to Avoid

应避免的事项

A few things are important to avoid when dealing with a loved one’s illness. Although I’m sure you can think of others, three of the main ones I’ve received feedback on are the following:

在照顾患病亲人时，有几件事需要特别注意。虽然我相信您还能想到其他的，但我收到的反馈中，最主要的三点是：

Comparing Conditions to Your Own or Those of Someone You Know

将自身情况或您认识的人的情况进行比较

The last thing anyone who is sick or caring for the sick needs to hear is how you or your aunt, cousin, or friend had this same condition, had major complications, and died from it. You likely have the best intentions, but try to keep your conversations as positive and hopeful as possible. Don’t tell them how they will feel or what they will experience. Everyone is different, and different people may respond to treatment differently. While someone might have had a horrible experience with a certain kind of treatment or therapy, that doesn’t mean everyone will. Some people never have any harsh side effects at all, so don’t offer your stories of gloom and doom as they will do so much more harm than good.

对于任何生病或照顾病人的人来说，最不需要听到的就是你或你的姑姑、表亲或朋友曾经患过同样的疾病，并因此出现严重并发症甚至去世。你或许出于好意，但请尽量保持积极乐观的态度。不要告诉他们自己会有什么感受或经历。每个人的情况都不同，对治疗的反应也不同。虽然有人可能在接受某种治疗或疗法时有过糟糕的经历，但这并不意味着每个人都会如此。有些人甚至完全不会出现任何严重的副作用，所以不要讲述那些悲观绝望的故事，它们只会弊大于利。

Beth recalls how unsettling one such conversation was after she was diagnosed with breast cancer. “Someone whose friend had a double mastectomy told me what I was going to experience, as in ‘You’re going to be depressed. You’re going to mourn for your lost body for years. You’re going to feel powerless.’ It was wrong on several levels. She herself had not been through it. She was merely playing armchair therapist.”

贝丝回忆起确诊乳腺癌后，一次类似的谈话让她感到非常不安。“一个朋友做过双侧乳房切除手术的人告诉我我将会经历什么，比如‘你会抑郁的。你会为失去的身体悲伤好几年。你会感到无能为力。’这在很多方面都是错误的。她自己并没有经历过这些。她只不过是在扮演纸上谈兵的心理咨询师。”

Needless to say, this is a time when people are scared and need words of encouragement, not horror stories of what someone else experienced. So take the advice my mother always used to give me as a child: “If you can’t say something nice, don’t say anything at all.” A good rule of thumb in these types of situations might be “If you don’t have something positive to share, then zip your lips.” But if you do have a story of success, healing, and growth, please share! These stories can be very encouraging as they offer HOPE!

毋庸置疑，此时此刻，人们感到恐惧，需要的是鼓励的话语，而不是别人的恐怖经历。所以，请记住我母亲从小就教导我的那句话：“如果说不出好话，那就什么都别说。” 在这种情况下，一条很好的原则或许是：“如果你没有什么积极的事情可以分享，那就闭嘴。” 但如果你确实有关于成功、治愈和成长的故事，请务必分享！这些故事能给人带来希望，非常鼓舞人心！

Not Believing Their Condition Is Real

不相信他们的病情是真实的

Some people think that glossing over a diagnosis might be a way to actually help the person affected. They often think, If I don’t act like it’s a big deal around them, maybe they won’t feel so bad about it. Some have even been so bold as to accuse the doctors of being wrong or to suggest that the diagnosis is incorrect.

有些人认为，淡化诊断结果或许反而能帮助患者。他们常常想：如果我不在他们面前表现得好像这件事很严重，也许他们就不会那么难过了。有些人甚至大胆地指责医生诊断错误，或者暗示诊断本身就是错误的。

Regardless of your intentions, please refrain. Minimizing a condition can be especially frustrating to someone who is dealing with a mental illness such as depression. It is not helpful to say things like “Just get over it!” or “What do you have to be depressed about?” or even “Count your blessings.” Don’t you think if they could “just get over it,” they would? Depression is so much more than just being in a funk. Depression can be an extremely debilitating disease that sometimes makes getting out of bed a major accomplishment. It can also lead to many other illnesses and conditions, and it usually can’t be cured with just a pill.

无论你的初衷是什么，请不要轻视他们的病情，这对正在与抑郁症等精神疾病作斗争的人来说尤其令人沮丧。诸如“振作起来！”、“你有什么好抑郁的？”甚至“知足常乐”之类的话都无济于事。难道你认为如果他们真的能“振作起来”，他们就不会吗？抑郁症远不止是情绪低落那么简单。它是一种极其严重的疾病，有时甚至连起床都成了巨大的挑战。抑郁症还会引发许多其他疾病，而且通常无法仅靠药物治愈。

Making Jokes about Their Condition

拿自己的病情开玩笑

Even if you have the best intentions and try to use humor as a pick-me-up, you might want to think twice about making comments that are meant to be funny. There’s a good chance it’s just too soon for jokes.

即使你出于好意，想用幽默来活跃气氛，你也应该三思而后行，不要轻易说一些刻意搞笑的话。很有可能现在开玩笑还为时过早。

After returning to work following cancer treatment, for example, Doug was actually told, “You don’t look sick; you just wanted a vacation.” Even though he knew the person was joking, he found it a very insensitive thing to say. Likewise, Beth, who’d just had a double mastectomy, said that insensitive comments like “Now you’ll have great boobs when you’re older” really bothered her, despite knowing it was a bleak attempt at cheering her up.

例如，道格在接受癌症治疗后重返工作岗位时，竟然有人对他说：“你看上去不像生病了，你只是想休假而已。”尽管他知道对方是在开玩笑，但他仍然觉得这种话非常不体贴。同样，刚刚做了双侧乳房切除手术的贝丝也表示，像“你老了以后胸部会很漂亮”这样不体贴的评论让她很受伤，尽管她知道这只是对方想安慰她的一种苍白无力的尝试。

While laughter is usually the best medicine, try to be aware that the patient might not be ready for personal jokes yet.

虽然笑一笑通常是最好的良药，但也要意识到病人可能还没准备好接受私人玩笑。

4. Helping in Times of Divorce and Unemployment

4. 在离婚和失业时期提供帮助

Divorce (or separation) and unemployment are two other potentially devastating situations that people face every day. I have several suggestions that I think you’ll find useful for helping friends in either of these situations.

离婚（或分居）和失业是人们每天都会面临的另外两种可能造成毁灭性打击的境况。我有一些建议，相信你一定能用得上，帮助那些身处这两种困境的朋友。

Divorce

离婚

Helping a friend who is going through a divorce can be very challenging for many reasons. While people don’t choose to get sick or die, at least one of the partners involved does choose to get a divorce or separation. And despite how accepting modern society seems to be, there still can be a stigma attached to someone who is divorced. Divorced people may be seen as cheaters, gold diggers, liars, losers, victims, etc.

帮助正在经历离婚的朋友可能非常困难，原因有很多。虽然人们无法选择生病或死亡，但至少其中一方会选择离婚或分居。尽管现代社会看似包容，但离婚人士仍然可能面临一些社会偏见。离婚人士可能会被视为骗子、拜金女、说谎者、失败者、受害者等等。

Complicated family issues can arise too. Family support might not be there as expected, and that can be very hurtful. In addition, friends and family often feel they have to choose a side, even if they’re close with both spouses. This can be uncomfortable for those who want to remain friends with both people and don’t want to be caught in the crossfire.

复杂的家庭问题也可能随之而来。家人的支持可能无法如预期般到位，这会让人非常伤心。此外，即使与夫妻双方关系密切，朋友和家人也常常感到必须选边站队。这会让那些既想与双方都保持朋友关系，又不想卷入纷争的人感到很不自在。

Divorce can be uncomfortable for the divorcing couple because they may be dealing with feelings of loss, anger, failure, and guilt. The feelings experienced during the process of a divorce can vary from day to day. One day, someone experiences loss and sorrow, and the next day, they may feel free and happy that the burden of a broken marriage has been lifted off their shoulders. A good friend is someone who will be there for them at their lowest lows and their highest highs, keeping an even temperament each time.

离婚对离婚双方来说可能非常痛苦，因为他们可能要面对失落、愤怒、失败和内疚等复杂的情绪。离婚过程中，双方的感受每天都可能有所不同。今天，他们可能沉浸在失落和悲伤之中；明天，他们可能就会感到轻松和快乐，仿佛卸下了婚姻破裂的重担。真正的朋友会在他们最沮丧的时候陪伴在他们身边，无论他们处于人生的低谷还是巅峰，始终保持冷静和包容。

While some people see divorce as the best thing that has ever happened to them, others see it as a very painful event. Some people said having their ex-spouse actually die would be much easier to deal with because they would be gone. With divorce, their ex is alive and there’s a chance of running into them and having to relive the pain each time. And when children are involved, these interactions with an ex-spouse can last a lifetime.

有些人认为离婚是他们人生中最美好的事情，而另一些人则认为离婚是一件极其痛苦的事情。有人说，如果前配偶真的去世，反而更容易接受，因为那样他们就彻底消失了。离婚后，前配偶仍然活着，你有可能再次遇到他们，每次都得重温痛苦。如果涉及到孩子，与前配偶的这种互动可能会持续一生。

So, much care is needed to help those who find divorce painful through this tough time. Here are some things that could really help someone you care about when they are going through a divorce or separation:

因此，我们需要给予很多关怀，帮助那些在离婚过程中感到痛苦的人度过这段艰难时期。以下几点建议或许能真正帮助您关心的人度过离婚或分居时期：

• Be there for them. Although Keith’s divorce was amicable, adjusting to it was still tough for him. He found having friends who were always willing to listen to him was very helpful. He also appreciated when his friends kept him busy with fun things to keep his mind off the divorce.

• 多关心他们。虽然基思的离婚是和平的，但对他来说适应起来仍然很艰难。他发现，有朋友愿意倾听他的心声对他帮助很大。他也非常感激朋友们带他去做一些有趣的事情，让他暂时忘记离婚的痛苦。

• Spend the night or invite them to spend the night with you. When Paula was going through her divorce, the hardest time for her was at night. “For me,” she said, “it was difficult to be alone. It sounds strange, but nighttime or darkness was the absolute worst.”

• 要么留宿，要么邀请他们来你家过夜。宝拉离婚时，最难熬的是夜晚。“对我来说，”她说，“最难熬的就是独处。听起来很奇怪，但夜晚或黑暗绝对是最难熬的。”

The thought of being alone at night can be frightening or nerve wracking for many, especially for women who have never lived on their own before. Some may have moved from their parents’ house or an apartment with friends directly into the home they shared with their spouse, and so they find themselves constantly on edge. For others, the long, dark nights give them too much time to think. Regardless of the reason, spending the night with a friend can provide them with a welcome break from their loneliness.

对许多人来说，独自一人在夜晚会感到害怕或焦虑，尤其是对那些从未独自生活过的女性而言。有些人可能刚从父母家或与朋友合租的公寓搬进与伴侣共同居住的房子，因此她们常常感到紧张不安。对另一些人来说，漫长的黑夜会让她们有太多时间胡思乱想。无论原因如何，与朋友共度一夜都能让她们暂时摆脱孤独。

• Invite them to hang out. Running errands together might sound mundane to you, but to a friend who is lonely, it can be a welcome invitation to get out of the house, spend time with a friend, and shift their focus for a while.

• 邀请他们一起出去玩。一起跑腿办事听起来可能很普通，但对于孤独的朋友来说，这可能是一个受欢迎的邀请，让他们可以走出家门，和朋友共度时光，暂时转移注意力。

• Listen without judgment. Many facing divorce constantly watch what they say and how they act in anticipation of how others will judge them. But almost every single person who responded to this survey said they needed someone to listen to them vent without the fear of being judged.

• 倾听而不加评判。许多面临离婚的人总是小心翼翼地注意自己的言行，生怕别人会如何评价自己。但几乎所有参与这项调查的人都表示，他们需要有人倾听他们的倾诉，而不用担心被评判。

Misty remembers that when she was going through her divorce, it was very comforting when her brother said to her, “‘I don’t care what happened or who did what to whom; I just want you to know that my heart is breaking for you.’ When he said those words, I knew he wasn’t passing judgment or trying to fix things. Instead, he acknowledged my traumatic experience and showed genuine, unbiased empathy.” So remember, it’s neither your job nor your business to determine who is right or wrong in this kind of instance. Your role is to be a friend by opening your ears and your heart. Listen.

米斯蒂记得，在她经历离婚的时候，哥哥对她说：“我不在乎发生了什么，也不在乎谁对谁做了什么；我只想让你知道，我为你感到心碎。” 这句话让她感到非常安慰。他说这话的时候，我知道他不是在评判我，也不是想试图解决问题。相反，他理解我的痛苦经历，并展现了真诚、不带偏见的同理心。所以请记住，在这种情况下，判断谁对谁错既不是你的责任，也不是你的事。你的角色是做一个朋友，敞开你的耳朵和心扉。倾听。

• Help them move or settle in to a new place. During a divorce, one or both people in the marriage will be moving out of their home and setting up their life somewhere else. Offering to help them move can be beneficial in many ways. Extra bodies are always welcome for moving heavy furniture, but packing up old memories is often agonizing, so a friend’s willingness to box things can alleviate some of the pain. And help in setting up their new household may be a great boost to someone who feels overwhelmed at this new chapter in life.

• 帮助他们搬家或安顿新居。离婚期间，夫妻双方或其中一方需要搬离住所，在新的地方重新开始生活。主动提出帮忙搬家有很多好处。搬运重物当然需要人手，但打包旧物往往令人痛苦，朋友的帮忙打包可以减轻他们的一些痛苦。此外，帮助他们布置新家也能极大地缓解那些对人生新篇章感到不知所措的人的焦虑。

Unemployment

失业

Your reactions to a friend or family member’s unemployment issues can be complicated, depending on how they lost their job. For example, you may react differently to a friend who was laid off due to downsizing than to a friend who was fired for inappropriate conduct. You may be more willing to help the friend who just caught a “bad break” than a friend who you feel might have “gotten what he deserved.” One situation might make you eager to reach out to help, while another situation might make you feel like running the other way.

你对朋友或家人失业问题的反应可能很复杂，这取决于他们失业的原因。例如，你对因公司裁员而被解雇的朋友的反应，可能与对因行为不端而被开除的朋友的反应截然不同。你可能更愿意帮助那些“运气不好”的朋友，而不是那些你觉得“罪有应得”的朋友。一种情况可能会让你急于伸出援手，而另一种情况则可能让你避之不及。

Either way, losing a job can be devastating. Because so many people define who they are by what they do for a living, losing a job, regardless of the reason, can strike a blow to their identity as well as their ego. For some, it may just be about the loss of income—the partial or complete loss of income for them and their family. For others, it can be a blow to who they think they are, who they’re trying to become, or who they’re trying to convince others they are. Therefore, in addition to worries about no money coming in, there may be feelings of extreme fear, anger, sadness, and even shame.

无论如何，失业都可能带来毁灭性的打击。因为很多人以职业来定义自我，所以失业，无论原因如何，都会对他们的身份认同和自尊心造成打击。对一些人来说，这可能仅仅意味着收入的损失——他们和家人的部分或全部收入的丧失。而对另一些人来说，这可能是对他们自我认知、他们努力成为的自己，或者他们试图向他人展现的自我形象的打击。因此，除了担心没有收入来源之外，他们可能还会感到极度的恐惧、愤怒、悲伤，甚至羞愧。

If you do decide to reach out and help someone in this situation, I have some suggestions that I think you’ll find helpful. The first and best thing you can do is to see if they want to talk about it. Then, be honest in asking what you might be able to do to help. If the person is open to having a conversation, you might be able to help them write a new résumé, or you might be able to provide a reference for them to use in their job search.

如果你决定伸出援手帮助遇到这种情况的人，我有一些建议，相信会对你有所帮助。首先也是最好的办法是，看看他们是否愿意谈谈这件事。然后，坦诚地询问你能提供哪些帮助。如果对方愿意交谈，你可以帮他们撰写一份新的简历，或者为他们提供求职所需的推荐人。

Not having a job probably means making different financial decisions—which means your friend might not be able to do the things they were used to doing. Just because they are cutting back does not mean you have to cut them out of your life. So continue to invite them out and let them make the decision whether to accept the invitation. While you might think it would be nice to offer to pick up the tab for dinner, be sure that is OK before you go out. Be clear in your invitation by asking if they would allow you to treat them to dinner. You might also want to offer a less expensive option, such as going to lunch instead of dinner.

失业可能意味着你需要做出不同的财务决策——这意味着你的朋友可能无法再像以前那样生活了。仅仅因为他们减少了开支，并不意味着你必须和他们断绝联系。所以，继续邀请他们出去玩，让他们自己决定是否接受邀请。虽然你可能觉得主动提出请客吃饭很贴心，但在出门前一定要确认他们是否同意。在邀请函中明确询问他们是否愿意让你请客吃饭。你也可以提供一个更经济的选择，比如一起吃午餐而不是晚餐。

Jody, who lost her job, said she felt very offended when friends would ask her to go to a movie and then pick up the tab. She said it made her feel as if she couldn’t afford it, yet she found it just as insulting when friends wouldn’t ask her out because they thought she couldn’t afford to pay. So while you may have the best of intentions when offering to treat someone to a movie or dinner, you could easily offend them in the process. It may be best to set the ground rules before you go.

失业的乔迪说，朋友们邀请她去看电影，然后自己买单，这让她感到非常受辱。她说，这让她觉得自己好像付不起钱似的。然而，当朋友们因为觉得她付不起钱而不约她出去时，她也觉得同样受到了侮辱。所以，即使你出于好意想请别人看电影或吃饭，也很容易在这个过程中冒犯到对方。最好在出门前就事先商量好规则。

You can bypass these complications if you cook dinner for your friend or host a night of family fun. You can even offer to babysit so your friend can apply for jobs or go for interviews. You may also want to treat someone looking for new employment with a trip to the salon for a haircut and color or a manicure, as these might be some of the first luxuries cut from a budget.

如果你为朋友做顿饭，或者组织一次家庭聚会，就能避免这些麻烦。你甚至可以主动提出帮忙照看孩子，这样朋友就可以安心地去申请工作或参加面试。你也可以考虑带正在找新工作的朋友去理发店剪个头发、染个发或者做个美甲，因为这些可能是他们最先要削减的开支。

Again, in this kind of situation, offering your friend or family member an honest, loving, caring conversation about how you can best support them might be the greatest thing you can do for them.

再次强调，在这种情况下，与你的朋友或家人进行一次真诚、充满爱意和关怀的谈话，谈谈你如何才能最好地支持他们，这可能是你能为他们做的最好的事情。

Conclusion

结论

The entire reason for writing this book was to eradicate the excuse “I don’t know what to say or do.” That excuse does not get you off the hook. That excuse ruins relationships.

我写这本书的全部目的就是为了彻底消除“我不知道该说什么或做什么”这种借口。这种借口并不能让你逃脱责任，反而会破坏人际关系。

The suggestions you have just read have helped the people who have been kind enough to share the knowledge they’ve gained from their own painful experiences. So now that you’ve read through all the suggestions, go back and pick one or two that either feel natural or comfortable to you or that you know will mean something to your loved one who is suffering. Some of the suggestions might even trigger another great idea you can implement.
